Peoples of Calderra

Playing a Marcher

The people of the March of Thorns

This is what your character knows growing up in the March. It's your people's story as they tell it. Every people's history has a few pages it would rather skip.

Who you are

You are a Marcher, from the cold grasslands of the north, where the March of Thorns runs up against the great wall. Lowlanders call you wallborn, half admiring and half pitying. The dwarves call your people "stone-debtors," which you'd rather not discuss.

Three hundred years ago, when the first emperor's armies marched north, the giants came down through the Thorn Gap. A knight named Corwen Ironhart held the gap alone for three days and three nights until the legion arrived. The emperor gave him the whole March with one order: hold it. House Ironhart has held it ever since, and so have you.

What defines you: you hold. Outsiders say you never let go of anything. They mean it as an insult. You don't take it as one.

Your home

  • The Thornwall runs along the north edge of the March with twelve towers and a chain of beacon fires between them. In three hundred years the beacons have never all gone dark at once.
  • The north is horse and sheep country, pine forest and frost, right up against the wall.
  • The south is the river country, where the Ford-folk farm rye and barley and fish the river at towns like Harrow's Ford.
  • For three hundred years the imperial treasury paid for the wall's soldiers. Since the emperor died, it hasn't. Everyone knows. Nobody says it in front of the duchess.

How you're governed

  • Duchess Brenna Ironhart rules the March: blunt, brave, and the only claimant to the throne who has actually fought anything. She held the High Tower through the worst winter in living memory.
  • The heir to the March is the eldest child, but only after being wall-proven: serving on the wall and holding a tower through a whole winter. An heir who fails is passed over.
  • Twelve Tower-Wardens, one for each tower, form the Thorn Council and advise the duchess. Her younger brother Garrick commands the High Tower.
  • Every Marcher serves three wall-years from the age of sixteen, noble or peasant, man or woman. You've done yours, or you're doing them now.
  • Desert your post and you get a thorn branded on the back of your hand and years as wallbound, hauling stone with the convicts. Commit treason and you're put out beyond the wall, unarmed, at dusk.

What you hold sacred

  • The wall, not as stone but as a promise.
  • The beacon fires. Letting one go out is as bad as desertion.
  • Your post. Wherever you're told to stand, you stand.
  • The long watch. The dead don't leave. When a Marcher dies, they're burned on a beacon fire and their ashes are mortared into the next repair of the wall. The Thornwall is built of your grandparents. On winter nights, when a beacon burns blue, the dead are walking the watch.

Your virtue: steadfastness. You stay, you hold, you don't complain about it. Your blind spot: nothing beyond the wall is owed mercy. Most Marchers would say that isn't a blind spot.

Life, food and ceremony

  • A normal day: a horn at dawn from the nearest tower, drill in the yard if you're on wall-years, then fields, flocks or forge. Evenings by the hearth with rye beer and stories about who held which tower in which winter.
  • A feast: black rye bread, mutton and barley stew, blood sausage, pine-smoked river trout, honey cakes at midwinter, and frostberry wine, sour and strong, from the berries that grow at the wall's foot.
  • A wedding: the couple stands a night watch on the wall together, side by side until dawn, then lights a beacon at sunrise while everyone in sight of it cheers.
  • A funeral: the body burns on a beacon fire, the shield is hung in the nearest tower, and the mourners say: "Stand easy. We have the watch."

How you talk

Sayings

  • "Light the next fire." (Do your part and pass it on.)
  • "North isn't a direction. It's a warning."
  • "The wall doesn't care how you feel."

Insults

  • "Southsoft": anyone who's never stood a winter watch.
  • "Deserter's hand."
  • "Fair-weather."

Superstitions

  • Never sleep with your back to the north.
  • When a beacon burns blue, say the name of someone you've lost.
  • Throw a stone over the wall before a battle, for luck. Sometimes one comes back.

What you think of everyone else

  • The clans beyond the wall: orcs and giants who have raided the March for three hundred years. You've buried people because of them. Most Marchers have.
  • The dwarves of the Ironspine: greedy hoarders sitting on mountains of iron, who claim the high pass and won't let anyone forget the wall's stone bill. Good smiths, though.
  • The Regency: in the old emperor's last years the wall's pay came late and short, and the duchess blames Regent Lysander for every coin of it.
  • Greenhollow: soft, well-fed farmers who sleep safe because you don't, and have never once said thank you.
  • The Saltreach Compact: merchants. Fine for selling wool and buying arrows. Count your fingers after.
  • The Order of the Dawn Lantern: the only lowlanders who show up when it's bad. You respect that.

Playing a Marcher character

Character ideas

  • A fresh wall-years recruit sent south on an errand, seeing the soft lowlands for the first time and unimpressed.
  • A wallbound convict halfway through a sentence, with a pardon waiting if they do something worth it.
  • A Ford-folk villager from Harrow's Ford who thinks the duchess cares more about the wall than about the people behind it.
  • A beacon-keeper who has spent years tending fires alone and talks to the blue flames.
  • A tower knight's younger child who will never inherit anything and has decided to make a name somewhere else.

Rules fit (no new rules needed)

  • Species: mostly human. Half-orcs live in the March too, and are watched closely on both sides of the wall.
  • Backgrounds that fit: Soldier, Guard, Folk Hero, Farmer, Outlander (a wall scout), Noble (a minor tower family), Criminal (wallbound).
  • Classes that fit well: Fighter, Ranger (scouts beyond the wall), Paladin (oaths of devotion or the watch), Barbarian, Cleric (the long watch), Rogue (wall scouts and smugglers).

Naming

  • Given name, and a place or family: Edda of Harrow's Ford, Tamsin Greyfell. Tower families take the tower's name: Owain of the Sixth Tower.
  • More given names: Bryn, Corra, Aldo, Wenna, Hollis, Merrin, Teague, Isolde, Ranulf, Sefa.

Not human? Anyone born in the March and wall-proven is a Marcher. The wall doesn't ask what you are, only whether you stayed.
