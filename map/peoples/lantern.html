Peoples of Calderra

The Lantern-sworn

The Order of the Dawn Lantern, and what it means to swear for a job

Every hero in these tales is Lantern-sworn for the job at hand. This guide is for anyone who wants to know what that means, or wants to play a full knight or squire of the Order.

Who they are

The Order of the Dawn Lantern is a small sworn order of about forty knights, from every people in Calderra. They are loyal to the realm, not to any claimant, and they don't take sides in the Age of Banners. Ordinary people call them lamplighters, fondly. Claimants call them "Maren's people," usually while trying to hire them.

In the first emperor's wars there was a night so dark the army couldn't march. A young knight lit a lantern and walked ahead of the column all night, through the burnt west, so the soldiers behind her could find the road. At dawn the emperor offered her a lordship. She refused: "I don't want the road. I want everyone to reach the end of it." He founded the Order around her lantern.

What defines them: light the road; don't claim it. Outsiders say they think they're above everyone else.

Being Lantern-sworn

  • The Order is too small to be everywhere, so it swears in heroes for one job at a time. That's you.
  • You swear to see the job through, to help anyone in need along the way, and to take no side while you're sworn.
  • At the end of the job you're released, and paid. Some Lantern-sworn are later invited to train as squires.
  • If you find something too important to carry (and in the Age of Banners, that happens), you hand it to the Order at the end of the job. It stays in their keeping, not yours.

The Order

  • Ser Maren Aldwyn, the Lantern-Captain, leads the Order: a Marcher shepherd's daughter and the first commoner ever chosen for the role. She's the one most likely to swear you in.
  • Lanterns are the full knights. Each carries a lantern lit from the first flame, which has burned in the Order's hall since its founding.
  • Wicks are the squires. Anyone from any people can become a wick at fourteen; after seven years of service, a wick is knighted.
  • The Lanternhold is their hall: a lone castle on a hill south of Solace, with no town and no land around it. It's the only neutral ground left in Calderra.
  • Rules: a knight who swears to a claimant, takes land or abandons someone in need has their lantern put out in front of the chapter, and leaves.

What they hold sacred

  • The first flame, and every lantern lit from it.
  • The road: getting people where they need to go, safely.
  • The oath: to the realm, never to a ruler.
  • The long gallery. When a knight dies, their lamp is hung unlit in the Lanternhold's gallery: three hundred years of dark lamps. A new knight chooses one to carry and learns its story.

Their virtue: service. Help, and ask nothing. Their vice: righteousness. A Lantern can be insufferable about being the only person in the room who isn't after something.

Life and ceremony

  • At the Lanternhold: lamps trimmed at dawn, drill in the yard, letters read at noon (requests for help from all over Calderra), and jobs handed out.
  • On the road: walk, help, write a report.
  • The Night of the First Lantern, the longest night of the year: a plain meal of bread, cheese and cider, eaten in the dark until the first flame is carried in at midnight.
  • Knighting: the new knight lights their chosen lamp from the first flame and keeps watch with it all night.
  • Funerals: the Lantern-Captain puts out the knight's lamp and hangs it in the gallery, and every knight present says the dead knight's last job aloud.

How they talk

Sayings

  • "Light the road; don't claim it."
  • "Someone has to walk ahead."
  • "Mind your wick." (Stay humble; also, look after the young.)

Insults

  • "Lamp-snuffer": someone who leaves others in the dark.
  • "Road-taker": someone who helps only to gain.
  • "Lordling."

Superstitions

  • A lantern that gutters for no reason means someone you're sworn to help is in danger.
  • Never light a lantern from a stranger's flame.
  • Leave a lamp in the window of any house you've helped. Villages all over Calderra do it; it's how the Order finds its friends.

What the Order thinks of everyone else

  • House Ironhart: brave and honest. Brenna calls the Order the only lowlanders who show up when it's bad, and she's right.
  • The Regency: Lysander can't command the Order, and it bothers him. The Order has never trusted a man who lies that well.
  • The dwarves of the Ironspine: oath-keepers. Respect, both ways.
  • The Saltreach Compact: good clients. They pay on time.
  • The Temple: thinks the Order should serve the Sun. The Order disagrees, politely.
  • The Ashen Pretender: the Order refuses to sneer at him. His herald has noticed.

Playing a knight or wick

Most heroes are simply Lantern-sworn. If you want to play a full member of the Order:

Character ideas

  • A wick a year from knighthood, sent out with a party of strangers to see whether they're ready.
  • A young Lantern from the provinces who thinks the Order should be doing far more than it is.
  • An old knight who has walked every road in Calderra and is tired of watching the Order stay out of things.
  • A Lantern-sworn hero who has been invited to become a wick, and hasn't decided.

Rules fit (no new rules needed)

  • Species: any. The Order recruits from every people in Calderra.
  • Backgrounds that fit: Knight, Soldier, Acolyte, Folk Hero, Guard, Wayfarer.
  • Classes that fit well: Paladin (devotion or the watch), Fighter, Ranger, Cleric, Monk, Bard (the Order's record-keepers).

Naming: knights use their own names with "Ser": Ser Tamsin of the Ford, Ser Orrin Vey. Wicks go by their given name.

Talk to your DM first. Playing a member of the Order puts you closer to the centre of things than an ordinary hero, and your DM may have something for you.
