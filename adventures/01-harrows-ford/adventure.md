# The Line at Harrow's Ford

> Everything below the header is DM-only. Only the header fields above appear on the player map (the cold open only once its act is current).

*A D&D 5e one-shot. 3–5 players, level 4, 3–4 hours. One uncommon item each (see `world/play-format.md`).*

> **Premise.** Harrow's Ford is the last bridge before the Thornwall. The imperial garrison was pulled north to the wall a week ago and never came back. Tonight an orc warband has made camp on the north bank, two hundred frightened people from the steadings between the river and the wall are camped beside them, and the reeve has barred the bridge gate. The Lantern-sworn have one night to decide what the bridge is worth. At dawn, the warband crosses, unless there's nothing left to cross.

## How the warband got here

<!-- secret -->
The clans live beyond the Thornwall, and the wall runs all the way to the sea at its west end. For three hundred years that was enough: the clans never had ships.

**The Ashen Pretender gave them some.** The Host's boats (the same western cove where Hargrove's ships land arms for the Heir; see `world/factions/saltreach.md`) carried Varn's warband around the wall's west end by sea and landed them on the Frontier coast. They crossed the river near its mouth, onto the strip between the river and the wall, and marched east along the north shore to Harrow's Ford. The wall's towers face north; nobody on them was looking behind.

**The voyage was a down payment, not an oath.** Ushra took the Heir's ships, and the price was this raid: hit the March hard enough that Brenna has to come south. But Ushra has **not** eaten from the Hearth's one loaf, the Host's binding vow. She is weighing the Heir's offer of the high pastures. Varn's fate at Harrow's Ford decides whether she does (see *Choice II*).

**The split on the north bank.** Two Host scouts with ash-grey faces came along to make sure the raid is worth the fare. They want the village burned. Varn reads his mother's orders as narrowly as he can: take the river line, pay the debt, burn nothing he doesn't have to.

**What this does to the refugees:** they are the steadings along the north shore, who fled east ahead of the warband until they hit the bridge and a barred gate.

**Why it matters later:** the heroes are the first people in the March who can learn the wall can be bypassed. And the boats are a thread into **The Black Gull** (Option B: Captain Vane's log holds the western cove).
<!-- /secret -->

## The site (DM map key)

North is at the top. The Gemini battle map (prompt in `world/maps.md`) should match this layout.

- **North edge:** fog, and the road north toward the wall.
- **North bank, west:** the **warband camp**, downstream, where the warband arrived after marching east along the shore. Varn, about forty orcs, the young giant and the two Host scouts.
- **North bank, east of the road:** the **refugee camp**, crammed against the north end of the bridge. Nothing guards that end.
- **The river** runs east to west across the middle, fast and cold.
- **The bridge**, in the centre: a **timber deck on seven squat stone piers**. The deck burns; the piers don't. Burning the bridge means firing the deck and leaving bare stone in the water.
- **The ford**, a quarter-mile downstream (west), between the warband camp and the village: waist-deep, rocky, marked by an old stone post. Wave 3 crosses here.
- **South bank:** the **bridge gate** (timber, barred, carts stacked behind it) at the south end of the bridge. Behind it, the village inside a low fence. The **mill** stands on the riverbank at the village's west edge, the first thing anyone coming from the ford reaches.
- **South edge:** the road south, where the Millhaven carter is pulled over.

The engine is the same one that worked at the Festival: early scenes earn rewards that change the fight. The difference is that here the players **choose what to prepare and what to give up**, so the fight they get is the one they built.

## Before the session

- **Send** the level (4) and the uncommon item list a week ahead. Have two or three pregens ready.
- **Ask each player one question** and weave one answer into the night (see *Personal motives* below).
- **Print** the job letter (*Handouts*). Every player gets one.
- **Map:** the Harrow's Ford battle map (prompt in `world/maps.md`). **If you use Gemini, start a new chat for this map.**
- **Tracker:** load the three waves as enemy groups; put Varn in last. Keep an index card per Edge so players can physically spend them.

### Personal motives (ask one per player)

Pick by the role each player tends to play, not by class.

- **Face:** "Someone on the south bank owes you money, a favour or an apology. Who?"
- **Brain:** "You've read about Harrow's Ford in an old book. What did it say happened here three hundred years ago?" *(Whatever they answer is a half-truth. Let it be.)*
- **Sneak:** "You've crossed this river before, without using the bridge. Why?" *(If they say anything at all, they know the ford exists. Give them the Staked Ford task for free.)*
- **Muscle:** "What's the longest you've ever held a position you were told to hold?"
- **Support:** "Who taught you to patch people up, and what did they say you'd never be able to fix?"

**Marcher PCs:** if anyone takes private hook 2 from `world/factions/ironhart.md` (*Ushra's mark*, the bone token), Varn recognises it on sight. That token is his mother's. Let it change how the duel and the choice feel.

## 1. Hook (10 min)

Default: the Order. Each PC has the same letter (*Handouts*), sworn in for this one job.

**Read or paraphrase:**

*"The road north runs out of trees about a mile from the river. From the last rise you can see all of it: a long bridge, a timber deck on seven squat stone piers, a mill wheel turning on the south bank, thatched roofs behind a timber gate. On the north bank, a sprawl of carts and lean-tos and cookfires, far too many for the open field they're crammed into. Beyond that, fog. Beyond the fog, the drums."*

A grain cart is pulled over on the south road, unable to go further. A lamp hangs from its pole. The carter is from **Millhaven**.

<!-- secret -->
**Running thread: a lamp in a window.** The carter hangs the lamp "for the Lantern-sworn who saved our Festival." He's hauling Greenhollow rye the March bought before the money ran out, and he's stuck because the reeve won't open the gate. **Rumour from another region:** "Funny thing back home. All the little ones are dreaming the same dream. A green light, down under the coliseum." (The Mind gem, leaking. See `ledger/state.yaml`. Don't explain it.)

His rye matters later: it solves the reeve's real problem (see *The Barred Gate*).

## 2. The face of the problem (20 min)

The heroes reach the bridge as the sun touches the river. Everyone on both banks goes quiet: someone is walking out of the fog.

**Read or paraphrase:**

*"He comes alone, onto the north end of the bridge, and doesn't stop until he's standing in the middle of it. A young orc, broad as a door, a greataxe slung across his back and a knotted cord wound around his left wrist. He drives a spear down between two deck planks. Bone charms clack on its shaft. Then he looks at the south bank, at the gate, at you, and speaks in clear, careful Common."*

*"I am Varn. My mother is War-mother Ushra. At first light my warriors cross this bridge. This river is the line. It was always the line. Burn your bridge before dawn, and we will take only what lies north of the water. Keep it, and we will take it from you."*

He turns and walks back into the fog. He doesn't look over his shoulder. The drums start again.

**What the table should take away:** the villain is a person, he is serious, he has terms, and **"burn the bridge" is a real offer.** Someone will raise it later (the reeve, at the third watch). It's also the first hint of the Thorn Pact: *the river is the line*.

<!-- secret -->
**What Varn actually wants (so you can play him straight):** the March to **accept the river as the line**. Burning the bridge isn't the goal; it's the proof.
- **If the Marchers burn it themselves,** they've conceded the border with their own hands, as close to a new pact as he can get without his mother and the duchess in one room. It also pays the Host's fare without a massacre.
- **If they refuse,** the bridge stays an open door onto clan land: the road Corwen's legion used. The deck can be rebuilt on its stone piers, so he has to *take* the crossing, hold the line, and burn it himself.
- **Why he doesn't just torch it from the north end tonight:** a fire he sets proves nothing (the Marchers rebuild next season, and the line is only his say-so). And he owes the Host a raid. Slip away after a quiet fire and the scouts report that Ushra's son ran from a village with no garrison. Fighting for the bridge pays the fare; he picks the narrowest fight that does.
<!-- /secret -->

If a PC calls out a question, Varn answers one, briefly, from the fog:
- *"What line?"* — "Ask your duchess."
- *"Why now?"* — "Because the fence-builders' soldiers have gone home."
- *"What about the people camped by the river?"* — "They are fence-builders' people, on the wrong side of the line. They should go south while they can."

## 3. Set-piece: The Barred Gate (social, 25 min)

**Spotlight: face.** The refugees from the steadings (shepherds, a few wallbound with thorn-branded hands, children) are on the north bank. The reeve, **Edda**, has barred the bridge gate and stacked carts behind it.

**Why it can't wait until morning.** The refugees are standing on ground Varn claims, and every way the night can go is bad for them:
- **If the bridge burns** with them on the north bank, they're stranded on clan land. The only way south is the ford: waist-deep, cold, at night, with children and the old, right past the warband's camp.
- **If the bridge is held,** they're camped at the bridgehead, exactly where Wave 1 hits at dawn.
- **The Host scouts want them.** Varn won't hunt refugees, but the scouts need Brenna dragged south, and two hundred dead Marchers would do it. The refugee camp is the easiest target on either bank.

Let the players work this out. If they don't, Edda can say it for them, bitterly: "Burn it or hold it, they're the ones on the wrong side."

### NPC: Edda, Reeve of Harrow's Ford

Sixties, iron-grey braid, one stiff knee from her wall-years. Carries a **tally stick** and notches it as she talks: sacks of rye, heads of sheep, mouths. Elected by the Ford-folk, who pay the March's taxes and take its raids, and who are tired of the duchess looking north. She is the session's warm anchor, its MAX: blunt to the heroes' faces, kind behind their backs, and the one who remembers them afterwards.

**What she says:** "Two hundred more mouths. A raider among them, likely. And a warband on their heels. You'd open the gate? Then you tell me which of my people goes hungry in the first snow."

### The progress track

Getting the gate open is a **three-step track**. Each success ticks a box. A failure doesn't end the scene, it costs time: **if the track isn't finished in three attempts, the scene eats the first watch of the night** (see *The Night Clock*). Any PC can try an approach; nobody repeats one that already failed.

| Approach | Check | Success ticks a box because… |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Find her real fear | Insight DC 12 | It's food, not raiders. Naming it changes the conversation. |
| Solve the food | Persuasion DC 12, or just pointing at the Millhaven carter's rye | The rye feeds two hundred until the duchess can send more. The carter agrees if a PC asks (and if anyone mentions Millhaven, he insists). |
| The Order's word | Persuasion DC 14 | The Lantern-sworn promise the Order will pay or petition the duchess. Edda doesn't trust lowlanders' promises, but she respects the Order. |
| Prove there's no raider | Insight or Investigation DC 14 (see below) | **There is one.** Finding him is the strongest move in the scene. |
| Shame or threat | Intimidation DC 15 | Works, and Edda remembers it. She won't consult the heroes at the third watch. |

<!-- secret -->
### The raider at the gate (the Ashen presence)

Edda's fear is half right. Among the refugees is **a young man with grey under his fingernails**: one of the **Host scouts** who came with the warband on the Host's boats (see *How the warband got here*). His job is to get through the gate tonight and unbar it from inside at dawn.

- **The tell:** he greets a PC **by name** before anyone has introduced themselves. Let it pass the first time. If a player notices, they've earned it.
- **Spotting him:** Insight or Investigation DC 14 (Perception DC 14 if they're watching hands). Advantage if anyone heard him use a name.
- **Caught:** he runs for the river; one Athletics or Acrobatics DC 12 grabs him. Searched, he carries **a copy of the heroes' own job letter, in a careful clerk's hand**, word for word (the mole clue; see `world/factions/dawn-lantern.md`). He says nothing about who wrote it. He does say: *"The Heir doesn't want your bridge. He wants to see how long it takes Brenna to come."*
- **Not caught:** he gets in. During Wave 2 he slips the gate bar, and Wave 3 gets **advantage on its first round** (see *Three Waves*). The letter copy is found on his body afterwards. If the gate never opened, he slips back to the warband in the night and briefs Wave 3 instead: same advantage, no gate bar.

Catching him ticks a box **and** gives the Edge **Unmasked** (below). The heroes now hold the session's mystery: *how did the Host get our orders?* Don't answer it.
<!-- /secret -->

### Outcomes

- **Gate opened (3 boxes):** the refugees cross. The militia gets bigger (unlocks *Drill the Militia* at full strength). Edda notches the stick three times and says nothing, which from her is gratitude.
- **Not opened:** the refugees stay on the north bank. Wave 1 sweeps through their camp (see *Three Waves*). Varn's warriors don't hunt them, but they're in the way of everything, and the Host scouts don't share Varn's rules.

**Everyone else's job:** brain reads Edda's tally stick (the numbers are the argument); sneak works the crowd for the raider; muscle moves carts and carries the old and the injured; support tends a feverish child (Medicine DC 10), which Edda sees.

## 4. Set-piece: The Night Clock (exploration and prep, 60 min)

The night has **three watches** before dawn. In each watch, **each PC does one task**. Several PCs can work the same task; only one needs to succeed. Success earns an **Edge** (an index card the players hold and spend during the fight). Each Edge can be earned once.

**Fail forward:** on a failed check, the player may **succeed at a cost** instead. Offer one: *it takes the whole watch and the next* (they lose their next task), *someone gets hurt* (the militia volley loses a die), or *noise carries* (the first wave gets advantage on its first attack).

### Tasks

| Task | Role | Check | Adv. earned |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Scout the north bank** | Sneak | Group Stealth DC 13 (half the scouts must pass); a scene, see below | **Forewarned**, and unlocks *Stake the Ford* |
| **Barricade the bridge** | Muscle | Athletics DC 13 | **Barricade** |
| **Rig the bridge** | Brain | Investigation or tinker's tools DC 14 | **Pitch Trap** |
| **Stake the ford** | Muscle, sneak | Survival or Athletics DC 12 (needs the ford found) | **Staked Ford** |
| **Drill the militia** | Face, support | Persuasion, Intimidation or Performance DC 13 | **Volley** |
| **Tend the old warden** | Support | Medicine DC 12; a scene, see below | **Old Warden's Bow** |
| **Rest** | Anyone | None | That PC gets a short rest's benefits |

Resting is a real task. A PC who rests in a watch gets short-rest benefits (Hit Dice, short-rest features) but adds nothing to the defence. Two watches of resting is a long time on a night like this; say so through Edda.

### The Edges

| Adv. | Spend it… |
| --- | --- |
| **Forewarned** | The party knows the wave order and composition. Advantage on initiative in every wave. Not spent; lasts the session. |
| **Barricade** | Wave 1 and the ram in Wave 2 must break it first (AC 15, 30 HP). Defenders behind it have half cover. Not spent until broken. |
| **Pitch Trap** | Once: a 10-by-20-foot stretch of bridge bursts into flame. Each creature in it makes a DC 13 Dex save, taking 3d6 fire on a failure or half on a success. The area is difficult terrain until the end of the next round. **Also makes the bridge burn fast** (see *Choice I*). |
| **Staked Ford** | Wave 3 arrives a round late and the ford is difficult terrain. Without this, nobody watches the ford, and Wave 3 surprises the party. |
| **Volley** | Once per wave, the militia loose together: one enemy takes 2d6 piercing, no roll. If the gate stayed shut, it's Ford-folk only: **once in total**, not per wave. |
| **Old Warden's Bow** | Once per wave, the old warden shoots from the mill roof: +5 to hit, 1d8+3 piercing. |
| **Unmasked** | Earned at the gate, not on the clock. The gate holds in Wave 2, and the party may ask the captured scout one question at any point during the night; he answers truthfully, then shuts up. |

### Scene: the north bank in fog (scout task)

**Spotlight: sneak.** The scouts wade or sneak across (the bridge is watched). **Group Stealth DC 13**; if fewer than half pass, they're spotted on the way back (see *Spotted*).

**What they find** (give them all of it if they get in; the fog makes it dreamlike):

- **The warband.** Around forty orcs, a young giant with a pine-log ram, and javelins stacked by the fires. This is *Forewarned*.
- **Stones in the river.** Young warriors throw stones into the water, for luck. *(A Marcher PC will recognise it: Marchers throw stones over the wall before a battle, and clan children throw them back.)*
- **The trophy.** A **helmet stuffed with straw** on a pole, painted with an Ironhart thorn. The warriors salute it mockingly and call it "the fence's guard."
- **The argument.** At the edge of the camp, two grey-faced figures with ash smeared on their cheeks are arguing with Varn in Common, the only language they share.

<!-- secret -->
The overheard argument (paraphrase, don't read it as a speech):
- **Host scout:** "Burn the village behind the bridge and the duchess has to come south. That's the point."
- **Varn:** "The river is the line. We take what was promised. Not one step past it."
- **Host scout:** "Your mother wants the high pastures? The Heir will give them to her. You'll want to remember whose boats carried you around the fence."
- **Varn:** "My mother paid for the boats with this raid. She didn't pay with that village."

The heroes hear the shape of it: someone is helping the clans, the help came with a price, and Varn is paying as little of it as he can. Don't explain the boats unless a scout is captured and questioned.

The straw helmet is a **straw warden**. On their march east, the warband climbed the westernmost tower **from behind**, from the south side the wall was never built to defend, and found it empty except for dummies in helmets. It is a **hollow tower**. A sharp player might work out that the wall has empty towers. Don't confirm it. Fenn (below) can, if pressed.

The grey faces are the session's Ashen presence. If the heroes didn't catch the scout at the gate, a captured scout here carries the job-letter copy instead.
<!-- /secret -->

- **The ford.** On the way back, they find (or are chased into) a wading place a quarter-mile downstream, waist-deep, marked by an old stone post. **Harrow's Ford is named for it.** This unlocks *Stake the Ford*.

**Spotted:** a chase back. Each scouting PC makes one Athletics or Acrobatics check (DC 12); a failure costs 2d6 from a javelin or a fall on the rocks. The warband now knows the bridge is defended, and the first wave gets advantage on its first attack. The chase finds the ford regardless.

### Scene: the old warden (tend task)

**Spotlight: support.** Among the refugees is a wounded old man in a faded warden's cloak: **Fenn**. Nobody knows why a tower warden was down among the steadings.

**NPC: Fenn.** Seventies, wiry, a deep gash in his thigh. Chews his words before he spits them out. He has walked the wall for fifty years and he is ashamed of something.

- **Medicine DC 12** stops the bleeding and earns *Old Warden's Bow*: he'll shoot from the mill roof with a longbow he says he hasn't missed with since the old emperor's day.
- **What he says freely:** "They came from the *west*. From behind the wall. On our side of it." He saw boats pulled up at the river mouth, and he ran east ahead of the warband to warn the steadings. Nobody believed him: there's no way around the wall. The heroes are the first people who might.

<!-- secret -->
- **What he hides (Insight DC 14, or show him the straw helmet):** he is one of the two old wardens who walk the circuit of the **hollow towers**, keeping the beacons lit and the straw wardens upright. The westernmost tower is one of them. When the warband climbed it from behind, there was nobody inside to light the beacon, and Fenn was a day's walk away lighting the next one. He begs the heroes not to tell anyone: "If they know the towers are empty, the wall's done. Not by orcs. By everyone."
- **What to do with it:** nothing this session. It's a secret the heroes now carry. It pays off later if Garrick, the Pretender or the Council of Banners comes up. Note in the session log whether the heroes kept it.
<!-- /secret -->

## 5. Choice I: The bridge (third watch, 10 min)

At the start of the third watch, Edda finds the heroes. She has pitch, axes and four strong Ford-folk.

*"He told us the price himself. Bridge goes down, he stays on his side. I can have it burning in an hour. Give me a reason not to."*

This is a decision with no perfect answer. Let the table argue it.

| | Burn the bridge | Hold the bridge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **For** | Varn keeps his word. No battle on the bridge. The village is almost certainly safe. | It's the March's last road north. Brenna's soldiers, wool carts and winter supplies all cross here. Refugees still on the north bank (if any) aren't abandoned. |
| **Against** | The road north is cut before winter. Anyone still on the north bank is stranded on land the clans now claim, with only the ford to get across. The duchess will be furious. Edda doesn't care. | People will die. Maybe some of them. |

**Arguments the NPCs make:** Edda: "The north's never done a thing for us." Fenn (if alive and helped): "You burn a bridge in the March, you're telling the wall it's on its own." The carter: "I'd like to go home eventually."

<!-- secret -->
**What burning really means:** the river was the border in the **Thorn Pact**. Burning the bridge honours Corwen's broken line, three hundred years late. Nobody at the table knows that yet. If they find out later, this choice will look different in hindsight. That's the point.
<!-- /secret -->

If the heroes **don't engage**, Edda decides: she burns it if they intimidated her at the gate, and holds it otherwise.

- **Burn it:** go to *If the bridge burns* (below). If they earned *Pitch Trap*, it goes up in minutes; otherwise it takes the rest of the night and there's no third watch.
- **Hold it:** go to *Three Waves*.

## 6. Set-piece: Three Waves (action, 50 min)

**Spotlight: muscle.** Dawn. The drums stop. Then the horns.

Varn does **not** fight in the waves. He stands at the north end of the bridge and watches, spear planted. *"A champion doesn't fight until the warriors have been answered."*

**Between waves** there's a breath: no rest, but each PC gets one free thing (drink a potion, stabilise someone, move, pass an item). The militia cheers or doesn't, depending on how it's going.

### Wave 1: The Rush

**What the warband is after in Waves 1 and 2:** the **south bridgehead**, not the village. They want the defenders driven off the deck so they can fire it end to end. Varn's warriors **stop at the bridge gate**. Only the Host scouts and a few hotheads will go past it.

**A clever play:** a party that falls back behind the gate finds Varn's warriors hesitating to follow, and they won't press through without the scouts egging them on. Reward anyone who listened to Varn. The cost: giving up the deck means the orcs start firing it (see *Losing the deck*).

**5 orcs** pour across the bridge.
- **Barricade:** they spend their first round breaking it, at half cover for the defenders. **Pitch Trap** is at its best here.
- **Gate shut:** the rush comes through the refugee camp. Have two refugees caught in the open each round; a PC who spends their move getting them clear earns the militia's loyalty (narratively, and one free extra *Volley* if they have it).
- **Spotted while scouting:** the orcs get advantage on their first attack.

### Wave 2: The Ram

**The young giant** (use **ogre** stats) with a pine-log ram, **2 orcs** escorting, and **2 more** on the north bank throwing javelins (+5 to hit, 1d6+3, range 30/120).
- **Objective:** the ram is for the **barricade on the deck** (no attack roll; 2d8+4 to the barricade). With the barricade down, the orcs start firing the deck.
- **If there's no barricade,** or the party has fallen back behind the gate, the javelin orcs and a Host scout goad the giant into ramming the **gate** instead (AC 15, 30 HP). If it breaks, Varn's orcs stop at the gap, but Wave 3 has a way in through the front.
- **Raider not caught:** the gate bar slips open from inside this wave. The ram goes straight in.
- **Unmasked:** the gate holds no matter what.

### Losing the deck

If the orcs hold the deck at the end of any wave, they get torches into the planks at the north end. The fire spreads one pier-span (about 20 feet) per round toward the south bank. The party can put out a burning section with an action and a DC 12 Athletics check (buckets from the river, or by kicking the burning planks into the water). If the fire reaches the south end, the bridge is lost: the warband has burned it by force. Run that as the **champion lost** outcome, minus the duel: Varn raises his spear and goes west.

### Wave 3: The Ford

**3 Host scouts** (use **scout** stats; grey ash on their faces) and **2 orcs** cross at the ford downstream to hit the village from behind and fire the mill. These are the Host's people, not Varn's; this is the scouts' plan, not his.
- **Staked Ford:** they arrive a round late in difficult terrain.
- **No one watching the ford:** they **surprise** the party, and the mill catches fire in round 1. Put it out with an action and a DC 12 Athletics check, or watch the Ford-folk's winter flour burn.
- **Raider not caught:** they have **advantage on their first round**, because he told them exactly where the defenders stand.
- **Morale:** the scouts are spies, not soldiers. When two drop, the third runs.

### Tuning the waves

- **Running long or the party is hurting:** cut the javelin orcs from Wave 2. Wave 3 is the story wave; keep it.
- **Party is cruising:** add 2 orcs to Wave 1. Don't touch Wave 3.
- **A wave fails** (the party is driven back, or the village is breached): don't end the session. Varn calls the champion's ring anyway, to settle it by law before the Host scouts turn it into a massacre. The stakes go up: lose the duel and the bridge burns with the Host's people already inside the gate. Win, and Varn's warriors drag the scouts back across the river themselves.

## 7. Climax: The Champion's Ring (30 min)

When the third wave is answered, Varn walks to the centre of the bridge and pulls his spear out of the planks.

*"Your warriors are answered. Mine are answered. By the law of my mother's clans, the line is decided by champions. Send me one. If I fall, we go home the way we came. If you fall, the river is ours and the bridge burns."*

**Clan law** (Varn explains it if asked, which makes it a rule the players can lean on):
- One champion each. Each champion may bring **one second**, who can't strike but can heal, shield and shout. *(This is support's spotlight.)*
- The duel ends when a champion **yields or falls**. Varn will not strike someone who is down or who yields.
- The winner decides the loser's life.

**If the party refuses single combat** and rushes him: allowed. The clans see it as cowardice, and two orc honour guards join Varn (use the *whole party* row in his stat block). The champion's choice still happens at the end.

### The interference

<!-- secret -->
In round 2 (or when Varn first drops below half), **one Host scout** hidden in the reeds draws on the heroes' champion: the Host would rather a dead hero than a fair fight. This is the Host breaking clan law in front of the clans.

**Everyone else's job:** spectators watch. Anyone whose passive Perception is 13+, or who said they were watching the reeds or the treeline, spots the archer drawing. A PC who spots it can act once, out of turn: shout a warning (the champion gets +2 AC against the shot), throw something, or charge (the scout shoots at that PC instead).

**The shot:** +4 to hit, 1d8+2 piercing. If it lands, or someone calls it out, **Varn stops the duel.** He roars at his own warriors in Orc; three of them drag the scout out of the reeds. Then he turns back: *"There's no honour left on this bridge today. I yield."* Treat it as the heroes' win.

If no Host scouts are left (all captured or killed in Wave 3), skip the interference. The heroes earned that.
<!-- /secret -->

### If the champion loses

Varn doesn't kill them (clan law). He lifts his axe over their head, lowers it, and says: "You held long enough." The warband takes the north bank and burns the bridge itself, and the Host scouts slip away to report. The village behind the bridge is untouched. That's the failure outcome (see *The ripple*): the March loses its last bridge and Varn walks away alive, with no debt to the heroes.

### If the bridge burned instead (from Choice I)

Varn keeps his word. At dawn he stands on the north bank of the burnt bridge, raises his spear to the heroes, and turns his warriors west, back toward the boats.

The Host scouts don't keep anyone's word. They take **4 orcs**, the hotheads who wanted the village, plus **2 scouts** and their leader (use **bandit captain** stats; grey ash on his face; he is the face of the Host this session), across the ford at full light. That's the climax: a fight in the shallows and the mill-yard, with whatever Edges the party has (Wave 3's rules apply). Varn doesn't help them; he doesn't stop them either.

After it, Varn wades into the river up to his chest, throws the bone token across to the heroes (see *Handouts*), and goes west after his warriors. *"A clan pays its debts. Ask your duchess what Corwen signed at this river."* There is no spare-or-kill choice in this branch; burning the bridge was the choice.

## 8. Choice II: The champion's life (10 min)

Varn is down or has yielded. By clan law, his life belongs to whoever beat him. The bridge goes quiet.

**The pressure is real.** Marchers have a tolerated vice: *no mercy for the north*. Edda says "Do it." The militia say "Do it." If Fenn is alive, he says nothing and looks at the river. A Marcher PC knows what their people will think of them if they don't.

Varn says only: *"My life is yours. Take it or give it back."*

### Spared

He stands, unwinds the knotted cord from his wrist, and hands it to the champion (see *Handouts: Varn's cord*).

<!-- secret -->
*"My mother keeps the other half. Ask your duchess what Corwen signed at this river. Then ask her why she still keeps the pen."*

That's all he'll say about it. He doesn't explain the **Thorn Pact**, doesn't name it, and if pressed, says: "It isn't mine to tell. It's hers, and your duchess's." The full truth waits for Act II or III.

**Under clan law** his life is now the heroes'. "When you need the clans, send the cord north. My mother will know it." He also tells them, unprompted: "The grey faces carried us around your fence. My mother paid their fare with this raid. Tell her the debt is paid." He won't say how they came around.

Ushra counts the raid as her down payment settled. With her son given back to her by the Lantern-sworn, she doesn't go back to eat from the Heir's loaf, **for now**. She becomes a possible ally against the Heir (see *The ripple*). The Heir still has boats and still has the offer of the high pastures; he'll ask again.
<!-- /secret -->

### Killed

The militia cheers. Edda notches her tally stick. The orcs on the north bank go completely silent, then pick up their champion's body from the bridge without anyone stopping them, and carry it west along the river.

<!-- secret -->
Nobody says it at the table, but this is the moment the Ashen Pretender wins the clans. The boats were a down payment; Varn's body pays the rest. Grief makes the Heir's offer of the high pastures impossible to refuse, and Ushra eats from the Hearth's loaf. **Ushra joins the Host.** Her clans fight for the Pretender in Act III.

The cord is still around his wrist when they carry him away. The Thorn Pact hint is lost for now; it can only come from Ushra herself, much later, and angrier.
<!-- /secret -->

### Varn's cord (if spared or if the bridge burned)

A cord of knotted sinew, dyed in bands. At one end, bound in, is **a clay disc stamped with a wax seal impression: a thorn wound around a crown. The disc is broken clean through the middle.** Half of it is missing.

A Marcher PC recognises the thorn: it's the Ironhart mark. Nobody at Harrow's Ford knows why an orc would carry half of it.

<!-- secret -->
It's a clan-made impression of Corwen Ironhart's seal on the **Thorn Pact**. Ushra carries the other half, with the clans' copy. The cord itself is clan memory: each knot a generation since the pact was broken. Count them and you get roughly three hundred years.

## Designer notes

- **What's new versus the Festival:** players choose what to prepare, the villain shows up in the first twenty minutes, there are two real decisions, and the solo spotlights all have jobs for everyone else. NPC names start with different letters.
- **No stacked checks.** The gate is a progress track; the night is one roll per task, with success-at-a-cost on a miss; the scouting is a single group check. Nothing requires two successes in a row.
- **One reward per task.** Edges are the only rewards, and the waves are tuned for a party holding three or four of them. If the table is very lucky and earns six or seven, add orcs, don't remove Edges.
- **Reward the description.** A player who says *how* they barricade (overturned carts, the mill's spare grindstone) gets advantage. Same rule as the Festival.
- **Let Varn be likeable.** Big clear villains, grey politics: the grey here is that the warband's leader is the most honourable person on either bank, and the real enemy is the people who opened the door for him.
- **Keep the secrets small.** This session drops four hints (the river is the line, the broken seal, the straw helmet, the job letter) and explains none of them. That's the right amount for Act I.
