# The Line at Harrow's Ford

> Everything below the header is DM-only. Only the header fields above appear on the player map (the cold open only once its act is current).

*A D&D 5e one-shot. 5 players (scaling for 3–6 below), level 4, about 3 hours. One uncommon item each (see `world/play-format.md`).*

> **Premise.** Harrow's Ford is the last bridge before the Thornwall, and the bridge is bread: the Ford's mill grinds the March's winter flour, and every loaf the wall towers and the northern steadings eat this winter crosses it. The garrison was pulled north a week ago and never came back. Tonight an orc warband is camped on the north bank, and its champion wants the bridge burned. Not from his end: a fire lit on the north bank gets doused and rebuilt from the south. To burn it for good, the warband has to **cross, take the south bridgehead and set the fire there**. The Lantern-sworn and the Ford-folk have one night to get ready. At dawn, the warband comes across.

## The three threads (push these; let the rest come out only if asked)

1. **The river is the line.** Varn wants the March to accept the old border. His cord and broken seal point at the Thorn Pact. *(Payoff: Choice II, Act II/III.)*
2. **The Host opened the door.** Two Host warlocks, the **Cinders**, came with the warband, and the Host's boats carried it around the wall. *(Payoff: Ushra's choice, every later Host encounter.)*
3. **Someone told the Host you were coming.** A voice in the fog recites the heroes' own job letter. *(Payoff: the mole, `world/factions/dawn-lantern.md`.)*

Everything else in this file (the hollow towers, the straw helmet) is background. It's there if a player digs; don't push it.

## How the warband got here

<!-- secret -->
The clans live beyond the Thornwall, and the wall runs all the way to the sea at its west end. For three hundred years that was enough: the clans never had ships.

**The Ashen Pretender gave them some.** The Host's boats (the same western cove where Hargrove's ships land arms for the Heir; see `world/factions/saltreach.md`) carried Varn's warband around the wall's west end by sea and landed them on the Frontier coast. They crossed the river near its mouth and marched east along the north shore to Harrow's Ford. The wall's towers face north; nobody on them was looking behind.

**The voyage was a down payment, not an oath.** Ushra took the Heir's ships, and the price was this raid: hit the March hard enough that Brenna has to come south. But Ushra has **not** eaten from the Hearth's one loaf, the Host's binding vow. She is weighing the Heir's offer of the high pastures. Varn's fate at Harrow's Ford decides whether she does (see *Choice II*).

**The split on the north bank.** Two **Cinders**, the Host's warlocks, came along to make sure the raid is worth the fare (see *The Cinders* in `world/factions/ashen-pretender.md`). They want the March to bleed: a burned bridge, a broken village, dead heroes. Varn reads his mother's orders as narrowly as he can: burn the bridge, pay the debt, take nothing past the river.

**What this does to the steadings:** the farms along the north shore emptied two days ago, ahead of the warband. Edda let every one of them through the gate. They're in the village now, and most of them have picked up an axe.
<!-- /secret -->

## The site (DM map key)

North is at the top. The Gemini battle map (prompt in `world/maps.md`) should match this layout. *(If you're using the physical bridge, this is the layout around it.)*

- **North edge:** fog, and the road north toward the wall.
- **North bank:** the **warband camp**, west of the road. Varn, about forty orcs, the young giant and the two Cinders.
- **The river** runs east to west across the middle, fast and cold. Anyone who falls in is swept 20 feet downstream each round; DC 12 Athletics to grab a pier.
- **The bridge**, in the centre: a **timber deck on seven squat stone piers**, about 20 feet between piers, roughly 160 feet end to end. The piers don't burn; the deck does.
- **The south bridgehead:** a stone landing at the bridge's south end, with the **bridge gate** (timber, on iron hinges) and a timber pier-house under the deck where the Ford-folk keep pitch, rope and spare planks. **This is what the warband has to take.**
- **The ford**, a quarter-mile downstream (west): waist-deep, rocky, marked by an old stone post. The scouts use it to cross at night. The warband ignores it: Varn wants the bridge, not the village.
- **South bank:** the village inside a low fence. The **mill** stands on the riverbank at the village's west edge.
- **South edge:** the road south, where the Millhaven carter is pulled over.

The engine is the same one that worked at the Festival: early scenes earn rewards that change the fight. Here the players **choose what to prepare**, and the second watch lets them build their own, so the fight they get is the one they built.

## Before the session

- **Send** the level (4) and the uncommon item list a week ahead. Have two or three pregens ready.
- **Ask each player one question** and weave one answer into the night (see *Personal motives*).
- **Print** the job letter (*Handouts*). Every player gets one.
- **Map:** the Harrow's Ford battle map (prompt in `world/maps.md`), or the physical bridge. **If you use Gemini, start a new chat for this map.**
- **Tracker:** load the three waves as enemy groups, with the two Cinders in all three. Varn's card needs a damage tally (see *Champion's Challenge*). Keep an index card per Edge, with its tier written on it, so players can physically spend them.

### Personal motives (ask one per player)

Pick by the role each player tends to play, not by class.

- **Face:** "Someone in Harrow's Ford owes you money, a favour or an apology. Who?"
- **Brain:** "You've read about Harrow's Ford in an old book. What did it say happened here three hundred years ago?" *(Whatever they answer is a half-truth. Let it be.)*
- **Sneak:** "You've crossed this river before, without using the bridge. Why?" *(If they say anything at all, they know where the ford is.)*
- **Muscle:** "What's the longest you've ever held a position you were told to hold?"
- **Support:** "Who taught you to patch people up, and what did they say you'd never be able to fix?"

**Marcher PCs:** if anyone takes private hook 2 from `world/factions/ironhart.md` (*Ushra's mark*, the bone token), Varn recognises it on sight. That token is his mother's. Let it change how the challenge and the choice feel.

## 1. Hook (10 min)

Default: the Order. Each PC has the same letter (*Handouts*), sworn in for this one job.

**Read or paraphrase:**

*"The road north runs out of trees about a mile from the river. From the last rise you can see all of it: a long bridge, a timber deck on seven squat stone piers, a mill wheel turning on the south bank, thatched roofs behind a low fence. Torches are moving in the village: people carrying things, a lot of people, fast. On the far bank, past the bridge, there's nothing but fog. Beyond the fog, the drums."*

A grain cart is pulled over on the south road. A lamp hangs from its pole. The carter is from **Millhaven**, hauling Greenhollow rye to the Ford's mill: it's the wall's winter bread, and he hangs the lamp "for the Lantern-sworn who saved our Festival." He's not going any further tonight, and he's not leaving his rye either.

## 2. The face of the problem (15 min)

The heroes reach the bridge as the sun touches the river. Everyone on the south bank goes quiet: someone is walking out of the fog.

**Read or paraphrase:**

*"He comes alone, onto the north end of the bridge, and doesn't stop until he's standing in the middle of it. A young orc, broad as a door, a greataxe slung across his back and a knotted cord wound around his left wrist. He drives a spear down between two deck planks. Bone charms clack on its shaft. Behind him, at the edge of the fog, two thin figures in grey stand very still. Then he looks at the south bank, at the gate, at you, and speaks in clear, careful Common."*

*"I am Varn. My mother is War-mother Ushra. This river is the line. It was always the line. Burn your bridge before dawn, and we will take nothing south of the water. Keep it, and at first light we will come across and burn it for you."*

He turns and walks back into the fog. He doesn't look over his shoulder. The grey figures are gone before he reaches them. The drums start again.

**What the table should take away:** the villain is a person, he is serious, and he has terms. **The grey figures are not his.** It's also the first hint of thread 1: *the river is the line*.

<!-- secret -->
**What Varn actually wants (so you can play him straight):** the March to **accept the river as the line**. The bridge is the proof.
- **If the Marchers burn it themselves,** they've conceded the border with their own hands, as close to a new pact as he can get without his mother and the duchess in one room. It also pays the Host's fare without a massacre.
- **Why he has to cross to burn it:** a fire lit on the north end burns toward the defenders, who douse it span by span and rebuild the deck on its piers by spring. Lit from the **south bridgehead**, the fire takes the landing, the gate and the pier-house where the Ford keeps its timber, and burns the deck north toward his own people, where nobody can fight it. That's a bridge that stays down.
- **Why he doesn't slip across tonight:** he owes the Host a raid, and the Cinders are watching. A quiet fire proves nothing and pays nothing. A fight for the bridge pays the fare; he picks the narrowest fight that does.
<!-- /secret -->

If a PC calls out a question, Varn answers one, briefly, from the fog:
- *"What line?"* — "Ask your duchess."
- *"Why now?"* — "Because the fence-builders' soldiers have gone home."
- *"Who are the grey ones?"* — A pause. "Guests."

## 3. Rally the Ford (10 min)

**Spotlight: face.** Edda doesn't need convincing. She's been handing out axes since the drums started.

### NPC: Edda, Reeve of Harrow's Ford

Sixties, iron-grey braid, one stiff knee from twenty years on the wall. Carries a **tally stick**: tonight she's counting barrels of pitch and pairs of hands, and tomorrow she'll notch it once for every orc that goes into the river. Elected by the Ford-folk, who pay the March's taxes and take its raids. Loud, profane, generous, and the first one onto the bridge. She is the session's warm anchor, its MAX.

**What she says** (as the heroes walk in): *"Lantern-sworn? Good. Grab an end of this. That orc wants my bridge burned. That bridge is bread: every loaf the wall eats this winter crosses it. I'm not burning bread for an orc."*

**The rally.** Edda climbs onto the carter's wagon to speak to the Ford-folk and the steadings' people: shepherds, millers, a few wallbound with thorn-branded hands. She's good at shouting and bad at speeches. She waves a PC up beside her: *"You. Say something that isn't swearing."*

- **Persuasion, Intimidation or Performance DC 12** (advantage for a good line). **Success:** the militia starts the night with **Volley, tier 1** (see *The Night Clock*). **Failure:** nothing lost; they just start untrained.
- **Everyone else's job:** muscle hauls barrels to the bridgehead; brain counts what the Ford has (pitch, rope, planks); sneak watches the fog (a passive Perception 13+ catches a grey figure watching from the far bank, and it waves); support finds Fenn (below).

## 4. The Night Clock (exploration and prep, 50 min)

The night has **two watches** before dawn.

**How rolls work (both watches).** Every Edge task is a check against **DC 10**:

| Result | Outcome |
| --- | --- |
| 9 or lower | Nothing earned this watch. Try again in Watch 2. |
| 10–16 | **Tier 1** |
| 17+ | **Tier 2** |

Several PCs can work the same task; the best roll counts, and a PC who describes *how* they help gives the roller advantage. Each Edge is earned once; a higher tier later replaces a lower one.

### Watch 1: the work (rigid)

Each PC picks **one task from the table** and rolls. No resting in Watch 1: Edda has everyone on their feet.

| Task | Role | Check | Edge |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Scout the north bank** | Sneak | Stealth | **Forewarned**; a scene, see below |
| **Barricade the bridge** | Muscle | Athletics | **Barricade** |
| **Rig the bridge** | Brain | Investigation or tinker's tools | **Pitch Trap** |
| **Drill the militia** (with Edda) | Face, support | Persuasion, Intimidation or Performance | **Volley** |
| **Tend the old warden** | Support | Medicine | **Old Warden's Bow**; a scene, see below |
| **Study the grey ones** | Brain | Arcana | **Ash-Wise** |

There are six tasks and five PCs. Something won't get done in Watch 1. That's the point.

### Watch 2: the bridge (freeform)

The fog comes down (see *The voice in the fog*). Then the second watch is open. Each PC does **one** of:

- **Try an Edge again,** to earn one they missed or upgrade one to tier 2.
- **Walk the bridge.** The players know it's a bridge fight; let them learn it and **bring their own trap**. Tell them what's there (below), let them pitch an idea, roll it against the same DC 10 / 17 bands, and write it on an index card as a **custom Edge**.
- **Rest.** Short-rest benefits (Hit Dice, short-rest features), but nothing else. Edda says something about it.

**What the bridge has** (give this freely to anyone who walks it):
- **Loose planks.** Any span's deck can be unpinned so it drops when weight hits it.
- **The pier-house** under the south end: pitch, rope, spare planks, a block and tackle.
- **Pier ledges.** Each stone pier has a ledge just above the waterline, under the deck. Room for one Medium creature to hide, or to climb up behind the enemy.
- **The mill.** A grain hoist on the riverbank and a sluice that can be opened to make the current under the south spans faster.
- **The gate.** Timber on iron hinges at the south landing. It shuts, but it was built to keep sheep in.
- **The reeds** on both banks, chest-high.

**Custom Edge yardstick** (so players' ideas land at the same power as the table's):

| | Tier 1 | Tier 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Damage | 2d6 once, or 1d6 once per wave | 3d6 once, or 2d6 once per wave |
| Condition | One creature, save DC 12 (prone, restrained, knocked into the river) | Up to three creatures, save DC 13, or one with no save |
| Terrain | One span becomes difficult terrain | One span drops or is blocked for a whole wave |

### The Edges

| Edge | Tier 1 | Tier 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Forewarned** | The party knows the wave order and what's in each. Advantage on initiative in every wave. | Also **Marked the Cinders**: the party knows where the Cinders will stand. The first attack each round against a Cinder has advantage. |
| **Barricade** | Across the deck one span north of the landing. AC 15, 30 HP. Defenders behind it have half cover. | 50 HP. |
| **Pitch Trap** | Once: a 10-by-20-foot stretch of deck bursts into flame. DC 13 Dex save, 3d6 fire on a failure or half on a success. Difficult terrain until the end of the next round. | Two traps, on different spans. |
| **Volley** | Once per wave, the militia loose together: one enemy takes 2d6 piercing, no roll. | 3d6. |
| **Old Warden's Bow** | Once per wave, Fenn shoots from the mill roof: +5 to hit, 1d8+3 piercing. | Twice per wave. |
| **Ash-Wise** | The party learns the Cinders' rules: they can't harm anyone with their own magic, they flee with a spell that needs a spoken word, and their spells are leashed by concentration (see *The Cinders*). | Also: each Cinder has disadvantage on its first concentration save in each wave. |

**Pitch Trap and the burn:** pitch on the deck helps the warband too. If a rigged span is still unsprung when the warband takes the landing, *The Burn* (below) starts with one box already ticked.

### Scene: the north bank in fog (scout task)

**Spotlight: sneak.** The scouts cross at the ford and come up through the reeds. **9 or lower:** they're spotted on the way back. Each scout makes one Athletics or Acrobatics check (DC 12); a failure costs 2d6 from a javelin or a fall on the rocks, and Wave 1 gets advantage on its first attack. **Tier 1 or 2:** they get in and out clean.

**What they find** (give them all of it if they get in; the fog makes it dreamlike):

- **The warband.** About forty orcs, a young giant with a pine-log ram, javelins stacked by the fires, and **pots of pitch and bundles of torches** by every fire. They mean to burn something, and they mean to carry the fire.
- **Stones in the river.** Young warriors throw stones into the water, for luck. *(A Marcher PC will recognise it: Marchers throw stones over the wall before a battle, and clan children throw them back.)*
- **The argument.** At the edge of the camp, the two grey figures argue with Varn in Common, the only language they share.

<!-- secret -->
The overheard argument (paraphrase, don't read it as a speech):
- **A Cinder:** "Burn the bridge, then the village, and the duchess has to come south. That's what the fare bought."
- **Varn:** "The river is the line. We burn what was promised. Not one step past it."
- **The other Cinder:** "Your mother wants the high pastures? The Heir will give them to her. Remember whose boats carried you around the fence."
- **Varn:** "My mother paid for the boats with this raid. She didn't pay with that village."

The heroes hear the shape of it: someone is helping the clans, the help came with a price, and Varn is paying as little of it as he can. Don't explain the boats.

**If a player digs (background, don't push):** a helmet stuffed with straw sits on a pole by the fires, painted with an Ironhart thorn. The warriors salute it and call it "the fence's guard." The warband found it in the westernmost wall tower, which they climbed from behind and found empty except for dummies. Fenn can explain it, if pressed.
<!-- /secret -->

### Scene: the old warden (tend task)

**Spotlight: support.** In the mill, among the steadings' people, a wounded old man in a faded warden's cloak: **Fenn**.

**NPC: Fenn.** Seventies, wiry, a deep gash in his thigh. Chews his words before he spits them out. He has walked the wall for fifty years and he is ashamed of something.

- **The roll** stops the bleeding. Tier 1 or 2 earns *Old Warden's Bow*: he'll shoot from the mill roof with a longbow he says he hasn't missed with since the old emperor's day.
- **What he says freely:** "They came from the *west*. From behind the wall. On our side of it." He saw boats pulled up at the river mouth and ran east to warn the steadings. Nobody believed him. *(This is thread 2: the sea route.)*

<!-- secret -->
- **Background, only if a player presses (Insight DC 14, or shows him the straw helmet):** he is one of the two old wardens who walk the circuit of the **hollow towers**, keeping beacons lit and straw wardens upright. The westernmost was one of them. He begs the heroes not to tell anyone: "If they know the towers are empty, the wall's done. Not by orcs. By everyone." Note in the session log whether the heroes kept it.
<!-- /secret -->

### The voice in the fog (start of Watch 2)

The fog rolls south across the water and swallows the bridge to the knees. The drums stop. A voice comes out of it from somewhere over the river, pleasant and unhurried, and it says the name of one PC.

Then it recites a line of their job letter, word for word: *"...a warband is on the north bank, and the reeve asks for help keeping the Ford-folk alive until the duchess can send soldiers..."* Then, *"Use your judgment,"* with a little laugh. Then the fog lifts and the drums start again.

<!-- secret -->
That's the Hush (see *The Cinders*). It's thread 3, the mole: the Host has a copy of the heroes' orders. Don't explain it. If anyone asks Edda, she says the letter came by Order courier and she never showed it to a soul.

If a PC hasn't seen the Cinders yet, this also opens **Study the grey ones**.

## 5–7. The fight for the bridge

**Spotlight: muscle.** Dawn. The drums stop. Then the horns.

### The Burn (the warband's objective, every wave)

The warband wins by **holding the south bridgehead** long enough to set the fire there.

- At the end of each round, if **an enemy is on the south landing and no conscious defender is within 10 feet of it**, tick a box on the **Burn track** (three boxes). Orcs with torches throw them into the pier-house as they get there.
- **A defender can clear one box** with an action and a DC 12 Athletics check (buckets from the river, kicking burning planks into the water), or by driving every enemy off the landing (clears one box at the end of that round).
- **Three boxes:** the bridgehead is burning beyond saving. The warband pulls back north ahead of the fire. Run that as **The bridge burned** (see *The ripple*): Varn raises his spear from the north bank and goes west.

**A clever play:** fire lit on the deck north of the landing (by the Pitch Trap, or by the orcs) doesn't tick the Burn track. It's a problem for that span, not the bridge.

**Between waves** there's a breath: no rest, but each PC gets one free thing (drink a potion, stabilise someone, move, pass an item). Edda's moment is between Waves 2 and 3 (see *The dawn loaf*).

### The Cinders (all three waves)

The two Cinders come in with Wave 1 and stay for the whole fight. Stat blocks below. They know this fight matters, but it isn't the war, and they won't die for it.

**How to run them:**
- They stand **on the deck or the north bank, 60–90 feet back**, inside their spell range and inside the party's bow range. That's on purpose.
- They spend their big spells **when it counts**: on the party's best cluster, on the healer, when the warband is one push from the landing. Not round 1 of every wave.
- Each can hold **one concentration spell**. Hitting them is how the party breaks it.
- **They leave** (Scatter to Ash) when bloodied, when their wave is broken, or when they've done what they came for. They always keep a dimension door for it.
- **They can be caught.** Dimension door needs a spoken word. A Cinder inside a *silence*, gagged, grappled with its mouth covered, incapacitated or counterspelled can't leave. A Cinder dropped to 0 HP before its next turn is down. If the table pulls this off, let them have it: a captured Cinder carries **a copy of the heroes' job letter, in a careful clerk's hand**, and will say one thing before shutting up: *"The Heir doesn't want your bridge. He wants to see how long it takes Brenna to come."*

**A spell budget for the morning** (a suggestion, not a script; spend it where the fight is):

| | The Hush | The Elder |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wave 1 | *Silence* on the party's casters or healer when they cluster | *Transmute rock* on the stone landing: the defenders' ground turns to mud |
| Wave 2 | Second *silence*, or Stilling Word on a caster | *Confusion* on the defenders holding the line against the ram |
| Wave 3 | Holds *hold person* for the challenge (see *The interference*) | Ash Grip at will; leaves early if things go badly |

**Mud:** the landing stays mud for the rest of the fight (it's an instantaneous spell; the mud doesn't go away). That's a big deal at the place the party has to hold. A party that pulls the fight forward onto the deck, or lays planks over the mud (an action, one 10-foot square), is playing it right.

**Confusion:** confused PCs may swing at each other. That's the point, but don't let the orcs gang up on a confused PC.

**Why they never hurt anyone directly:** see *Pact of the Banked Fire* in their stat block. With *Ash-Wise*, the players know it; without it, let them work it out.

### Wave 1: The Rush (30 min)

**6 orcs** with torches pour across the bridge. The Hush and the Elder come behind them.

- **Barricade:** the orcs spend their first round breaking it, at half cover for the defenders. **Pitch Trap** is at its best here.
- **Spotted while scouting:** the orcs get advantage on their first attack.
- **Morale:** when four orcs are down, the rest fall back north. The Cinders cover the retreat and stay on the deck.

### Wave 2: The Ram (25 min)

**The young giant** (use **ogre** stats) with a pine-log ram, **3 orcs** escorting, and **2 more** on the north bank throwing javelins (+5 to hit, 1d6+3, range 30/120). The Cinders are still on the field.

- **Objective:** the ram is for the **barricade** (no attack roll; 2d8+4 to the barricade), then the **gate** (AC 15, 30 HP). Behind the gate is the landing.
- **No barricade:** the giant goes straight for the gate.
- **Pier ledges:** a PC hiding on a pier ledge (from Watch 2) can come up behind the ram crew. Reward it.
- **Morale:** when the giant drops, the escorts pull back.

### The dawn loaf (between Waves 2 and 3)

The sun's up. Edda limps down the line with a basket from the mill: last night's bread, still warm because the miller never stopped. She tears a loaf with her hands and passes it down the line, and the Ford-folk do the same.

*"Every one of you eats. Nobody fights the third one hungry."*

Each PC who eats may **spend one Hit Die** for free. If anyone mentions the Host's one loaf, or the grey ones, Edda spits in the river.

Then the horns go again, and this time it's one horn.

### Wave 3: The Champion (35 min)

Varn walks onto the bridge with his two **Shield-kin**, and pulls his spear out of the planks on the way.

*"Your warriors have answered mine. Now I answer you."*

The Cinders, if they're still on the field, are with him, and he doesn't look at them.

- **The Shield-kin** fight to keep Varn alive, and to put defenders in the river: the shield shove off the deck edge is their favourite thing.
- **Varn** goes for whoever is holding the landing, and plays it straight: he's here to take the bridgehead.
- **Clan law:** Varn and the Shield-kin will not strike anyone who is down, has yielded or can't move (paralysed, restrained by magic). The orcs have been told the same. Tell the players this the first time it matters; it changes how they fight.

### Champion's Challenge

Keep a **damage tally** on Varn's card: each PC's initial and a running total.

The first time Varn **starts his turn at 45 HP or less**, he doesn't attack. He plants his spear in the deck and points at the PC who has hurt him most.

*"You. By the law of my mother's clans, the line is decided by champions. If I fall, we go home the way we came. If you fall, the river is ours and the bridge burns."*

(Lost track of the tally? It's whoever took him under 45.)

**Clan law** (Varn explains it if asked, which makes it a rule the players can lean on):
- **One champion each.** The challenged PC may name someone to stand for them. Varn accepts, and says, *"You hid behind them."* The clans remember.
- **The champion's breath.** Before the duel begins, the challenged champion may spend up to half their Hit Dice. Varn doesn't. *("My blood is already on the bridge. Take a breath.")*
- **One second each,** who can't strike but can heal, shield and shout. *(This is support's spotlight.)* Varn's second is a Shield-kin, if one is standing; it does nothing but watch.
- **Everyone else steps back.** The Shield-kin lower their shields. The Burn track stops.
- The duel ends when a champion **yields or falls**. Varn will not strike someone who is down or who yields.
- **The winner decides the loser's life.**

**If the party refuses the challenge** and keeps fighting: allowed. The clans see it as cowardice. Varn fights on with his Shield-kin, and when he drops, Relentless Endurance leaves him on 1 HP and he yields. Choice II still happens.

### The interference

<!-- secret -->
In round 2 of the duel (or when Varn first drops below 20 HP), **the Hush** casts *hold person* on the heroes' champion from the fog. The Host would rather have a dead hero than a fair fight. This is the Host breaking clan law in front of the clans.

**Everyone else's job:** spectators watch. Anyone with passive Perception 13+, or who said they were watching the Cinders, sees the Hush lift her hand. A PC who spots it can act once, out of turn: shout a warning (the champion gets advantage on the save), shoot or throw something at her (a hit forces a concentration save the moment the spell lands), or charge.

**If the champion is held,** Varn stops with his axe raised. He won't strike someone who can't move. He roars at the Hush in Orc; both Shield-kin go for her, and she Scatters to Ash. Then he turns back: *"There's no honour left on this bridge today. I yield."* Treat it as the heroes' win.

**If the warning works and the spell fails,** the duel goes on, and Varn has seen what his guests are.

If the Hush was captured or killed, or has no *hold person* left, skip the interference. The heroes earned that.
<!-- /secret -->

### If the champion loses

Varn doesn't kill them (clan law). He lifts his axe over their head, lowers it, and says: *"You held long enough."* His warriors fire the bridgehead and walk back north ahead of the flames. The Cinders are gone. The village is untouched. That's **The bridge burned**: the March loses its last bridge and Varn walks away alive, with no debt to the heroes.

### If the players burn the bridge themselves

They can: Varn offered. Edda is furious ("You've burned the wall's bread"), but she won't fight the Lantern-sworn over it, and if they burned it from the south it's down for good.

At dawn Varn stands on the north bank, raises his spear to them, and turns his warriors west. *"A clan pays its debts. Ask your duchess what Corwen signed at this river."* He throws them his cord across the water (see *Handouts*); there's no Choice II in this branch.

<!-- secret -->
The Cinders don't keep anyone's word. They take **6 hotheads** (orcs) across the ford at full light to fire the mill: Wave 1's rules, at the mill-yard and the shallows, with whatever Edges apply. Varn doesn't help them; he doesn't stop them either. The party is fresh, so run it hard.
<!-- /secret -->

## 8. Choice II: The champion's life (10 min)

Varn is down or has yielded. By clan law, his life belongs to whoever beat him. The bridge goes quiet.

**The pressure is real.** Marchers have a tolerated vice: *no mercy for the north*. Edda says "Do it." The militia say "Do it." If Fenn is alive, he says nothing and looks at the river. A Marcher PC knows what their people will think of them if they don't.

Varn says only: *"My life is yours. Take it or give it back."*

### Spared

He stands, unwinds the knotted cord from his wrist, and hands it to the champion (see *Handouts: Varn's cord*).

<!-- secret -->
*"My mother keeps the other half. Ask your duchess what Corwen signed at this river. Then ask her why she still keeps the pen."*

That's all he'll say about it. He doesn't explain the **Thorn Pact**, doesn't name it, and if pressed, says: "It isn't mine to tell. It's hers, and your duchess's." The full truth waits for Act II or III.

He also tells them, unprompted: *"The grey ones carried us around your fence. My mother paid their fare with this raid. Tell her the debt is paid."* And if the heroes didn't capture a Cinder, he gives them a folded paper he took off the Elder's pallet: **a copy of their own job letter, in a careful clerk's hand.** *"They knew you were coming before you did."*

**Under clan law** his life is now the heroes'. "When you need the clans, send the cord north. My mother will know it."

Ushra counts the raid as her down payment settled. With her son given back to her by the Lantern-sworn, she doesn't go back to eat from the Heir's loaf, **for now**. She becomes a possible ally against the Heir (see *The ripple*).
<!-- /secret -->

### Killed

The militia cheers. Edda notches her tally stick. The orcs on the north bank go completely silent, then pick up their champion's body from the bridge without anyone stopping them, and carry it west along the river.

<!-- secret -->
Nobody says it at the table, but this is the moment the Ashen Pretender wins the clans. The boats were a down payment; Varn's body pays the rest. Grief makes the Heir's offer of the high pastures impossible to refuse, and Ushra eats from the Hearth's loaf. **Ushra joins the Host.** Her clans fight for the Pretender in Act III.

The cord is still around his wrist when they carry him away. The Thorn Pact hint is lost for now; it can only come from Ushra herself, much later, and angrier.
<!-- /secret -->

### Encounter math (level 4, five PCs)

Five level 4 PCs: hard is 1,875 adjusted XP, deadly 2,500, and the adventuring day is about 8,500.

| Wave | Enemies | Adjusted XP | Difficulty before Edges |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 6 orcs + both Cinders | 1,200 for the orcs | Medium by the numbers; **hard** in play, because the Cinders turn the orcs into a real threat. The formula would call it 5,000; ignore that, they deal no damage |
| 2 | Ogre + 5 orcs (3 escorts, 2 javelins) + the Cinders | 1,900 | Hard |
| 3 | Varn + 2 Shield-kin + the Cinders | 3,000 | Deadly on paper. Softer in play: the challenge ends the melee at half Varn's HP, clan law means no killing blows, and the dawn loaf comes first |
| Heroes burned it | 6 orcs + both Cinders at the ford | 1,200 | Hard, against a fresh party |

**Total for the morning:** about 6,100 XP, under the 8,500 adventuring day, with no long rest. A party holding three or four Edges (some at tier 2) should come through bloodied but standing. A party with none will be in real trouble by Wave 3, which is what the Night Clock is for.

**Scaling:**
- **3 players:** drop two orcs from each wave and one Shield-kin; Varn starts at 75 HP and challenges at 38.
- **4 players:** drop one orc from each wave and one Shield-kin.
- **6 players:** add one orc to each wave; Varn starts at 100 HP and challenges at 50.
- **Running long:** cut the javelin orcs from Wave 2. Keep Wave 3 whole: it's the story wave.
- **A wave goes badly** (the Burn track has two boxes and the party is down to the wire): don't end it there. Varn walks onto the bridge early and calls the challenge, to settle it by law before the Cinders turn it into a massacre.

### Varn's cord (if spared or if the heroes burned the bridge)

A cord of knotted sinew, dyed in bands. At one end, bound in, is **a clay disc stamped with a wax seal impression: a thorn wound around a crown. The disc is broken clean through the middle.** Half of it is missing.

A Marcher PC recognises the thorn: it's the Ironhart mark. Nobody at Harrow's Ford knows why an orc would carry half of it.

<!-- secret -->
It's a clan-made impression of Corwen Ironhart's seal on the **Thorn Pact**. Ushra carries the other half, with the clans' copy. The cord itself is clan memory: each knot a generation since the pact was broken. Count them and you get roughly three hundred years.

## Designer notes

- **What changed in pass 1:** the barred gate and the refugees are gone; the warband's goal is to burn the bridge from the south end, so every wave is a push for the bridgehead; Edda fights instead of arguing; the Night Clock is two watches, and the second lets the players build their own traps; the Cinders replace the Host scouts and stay for the whole fight; Varn fights in Wave 3, and the duel is something he calls mid-fight.
- **Three threads, not eight.** The river line, the Host's hand, the mole. Everything else is background for players who dig.
- **No stacked checks.** One roll per task, DC 10 with tiers. The rally can't fail badly. Nothing needs two successes in a row.
- **Reward the description.** A player who says *how* they barricade (overturned carts, the mill's spare grindstone) gets advantage. Same rule as the Festival.
- **Let Varn be likeable.** Big clear villains, grey politics: the warband's leader is the most honourable person on either bank, and the real enemy is the pair in grey who opened the door for him.
- **Let the Cinders be beatable.** They're recurring, so they should usually get away, but the rules for stopping them are real. A table that silences one has earned her.
