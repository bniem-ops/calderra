This is what your character knows growing up on the coast. It's your people's story as *they* tell it. Reachers keep honest books, but a story isn't a ledger.

## Who you are

You are a **Reacher**, from the harbours and salt marshes of the east coast, which your people call **the Reach**. Lowlanders call you **coin-gulls**, because you're loud, you're everywhere, and you're after their purse. In a toast, Reachers call themselves **"free hulls."**

The coast was nothing but salt marsh and fishing huts when the first captains came. They dug the harbours with their own hands, built ships from the eastern pines and traded with anyone who'd trade back. Everything the Reach has, it earned. The empire only ever showed up to collect taxes.

What defines you: **you pay your way.** Outsiders say everything has a price to you. You'd say that's just honesty about it.

## Your home

- **The Reach** is a long coast of deep harbours, pine forest and salt marsh. Every port has its own fishing fleet, shipyards and salt pans.
- **Salt fish** from the Reach feeds half of Calderra's armies through winter. **Reacher ships** carry almost everything Solace imports. The sea is the fastest road in the world, and the Reach owns it.
- The coast grows no grain. You buy it from Greenhollow and the Delta, and you always pay a fair price, which is more than the Regency's Granaries ever did.
- The week the emperor died, the Reach signed **the Saltreach Compact** and declared itself a free republic. No crown, no charter, no imperial tax collectors.
- The imperial navy went unpaid when the emperor died, and some of its ships turned pirate. The worst is **the Black Gull**, the old flagship, under **Captain Jory Vane**.

## How you're governed

- **The Tide Table** governs the Reach. Every captain who owns a ship has a seat, and votes are weighted by **tonnage**: a fishing boat is one vote, a great merchantman is forty.
- **The Speaker** chairs the Table and is elected each spring. Now it's **Guildmistress Odessa Reef**, a smuggler for twenty years before she was a stateswoman, and the woman who wrote the Compact.
- **The Old Houses** are the great trading families who ran imperial shipping for a century. They own about half the tonnage on the coast. Their head, **Magnate Pell Hargrove**, is smooth, patient and very rich.
- **The free captains** (small ship-owners, ex-smugglers and fishers) are Odessa's people.
- **Bond-sailors** work off a debt under a bond, usually five years. Finish your bond, buy a share in a ship, and one day you'll have a vote. That's the Reacher promise: anyone can rise.
- **Break a contract** and your name is painted on **the Wreck Wall** in every harbour. No Reacher will trade with you, crew for you or hire you until you make it right. **Piracy** against a Reacher ship gets you hanged at the tideline.

## What you hold sacred

- **A signed contract.** Witnessed, sealed and kept.
- **The ship.** She's alive. She has a name and a temper. You never rename a ship.
- **The tally.** Debts are settled, always, in the end.
- **The Long Tally.** The sea keeps accounts. If you die with your debts paid, you sink to rest. If you die owing, you sail forever in the fog until someone pays for you. That's why families settle their dead's debts before the funeral.

**Your virtue:** fair dealing. Honest measure, honest books, honest price.
**Your vice:** "it's only business."

## Life, food and ceremony

- **A normal day:** up with the tide bell; boats out before dawn; ships loading by first light; salt pans raked at noon. Afternoons on the docks, where prices are chalked on slate boards and deals are sealed with a spit and a handshake, then signed properly. Evenings in the harbour taverns: shanties, dice, rum and arguments about the Table.
- **A feast:** a whole fish baked in a salt crust and cracked open at the table, oysters, crab boiled in seawater, salt-pork pie, black bread and butter, and **dark rum**. The finest thing at a wedding is something from far away: dwarven cheese, Delta oranges, Greenhollow apples.
- **A wedding:** at sea, on the couple's own boat if they have one. The marriage contract is signed on deck and read aloud, each partner pours a measure of rum over the side for the sea's share, and then everyone sings.
- **A funeral:** debts first. Then the body is wrapped in sailcloth and given to the sea at dusk, and the name is painted on the stern of the ship they sailed on, beneath her own name, so she carries them.

## How you talk

**Sayings**
- "Square your tally." (Pay what you owe; also, do the right thing.)
- "Every tide has a price."
- "A free hull goes where it likes."

**Insults**
- "Short-weight": a cheat.
- "Landlocked": anyone stuck in their ways.
- "Barnacle": a hanger-on who never pays a round.

**Superstitions**
- Pay every debt the night before you sail.
- Never count coins at sea; it tells the sea you have something worth taking.
- A ghost ship in the fog is someone's unpaid debt. Throw a coin over the side.

## What you think of everyone else

- **The dwarves of the Ironspine:** your best customers. They keep good books, pay in good metal and never haggle twice. You respect them, and they respect you back.
- **The Regency:** Solace buys everything on your ships and still acts as if it's doing you a favour. The Granaries pay farmers too little and charge bakers too much. Reacher ships are happy to sell cheaper.
- **House Ironhart:** brave, grim and hopeless with numbers. Good for wool; bad for conversation.
- **Greenhollow:** honest farmers who like a fair price. You give them one.
- **The Prince-Merchants of Vareth:** rival bankers in silk coats. Old grudges over the southern sea lanes.
- **The Black Gull:** unpaid navy turned pirate. The Compact will pay a fortune to see her sunk.

## Playing a Reacher character

**Character ideas**
- **A bond-sailor** with a year left on their bond and a plan to buy a share in a ship, if they survive the trip.
- **A young captain** with one leaky boat, one vote at the Table and big ideas.
- **A Compact negotiator** sent inland to make deals the Table can't make by letter.
- **An Old House heir** who has decided to see whether they're good at anything besides being rich.
- **A former imperial marine** whose ship went pirate, and who chose not to go with her.

**Rules fit (no new rules needed)**
- **Species:** any. The Reach trades with everyone, and a Reacher is anyone born to a harbour.
- **Backgrounds that fit:** Sailor, Pirate, Merchant, Guild Artisan (shipwright, salt-maker), Charlatan, Criminal (smuggler), Noble (an Old House family).
- **Classes that fit well:** Rogue (smugglers and negotiators), Fighter (marines), Bard (shanty-singers and dealmakers), Ranger (coastal scouts), Druid (storm and sea), Warlock (a pact with something under the waves).

**Naming**
- Given name and family name, often a trade or a boat: *Mara Saltash*, *Benno Tarrow*, *Kit of the Whelk*.
- Old House families use their house name like a title: *Lysa Hargrove*.
- More given names: Nell, Roan, Wick, Sorrel, Pike, Meg, Callum, Dorrie, Fen, Hale.
