# Session 1 — The Line at Harrow's Ford

Private DM notes for this session. Held, but not the way the adventure planned it.

## What happened

- The party fought **Wave 1 and Wave 2** and got low. Varn then walked on early and called the Champion's Challenge, which is the "a wave goes badly" branch.
- **Sharewood** (Harengon chronurgy wizard) was the champion. **Joshua Shoemaker** (wild magic sorcerer) was the second.
- In the duel, Joshua **charmed Varn** and negotiated terms. The clans keep the land north of the bridge, and the bridge stays standing. The river is the line, but only in Varn's head.
- There was no Wave 3 and no Choice II. Varn was neither killed nor formally spared. He walked away believing he had won his mother's war.

## Aftermath (canon, between sessions)

This happened off-screen after the party left. It shouldn't change how any other one-shot runs. It turns up as echoes; see "Harrow's Ford echoes" in each adventure file.

### What happened, in order

1. **Varn holds his side.** The charm wears off on the north bank, and Varn knows. He keeps the terms anyway. To him the charm only made the southerners say out loud what Corwen signed three hundred years ago. The warband **barricades the north end of the bridge** and settles into the riverside steadings. Varn could burn the bridge from his own bank now, since it's his land. He doesn't, because the bridge standing is the proof the March accepted the line.
2. **Gromm's rider.** Before the party left, Gromm sent a Ford-folk rider south **to the Regency**, not the duchess, asking for soldiers to fortify the bridge. Brenna's garrison was gone and Solace was four days down the north road.
3. **The Heir moves in.** The Cinders report the duel to Kestrel. The Host's boats bring the north bank **pitch, timber, food and two more boatloads of warriors**. It costs the Heir little and gets him a lot:
   - The wall loses its bread road.
   - Brenna is squeezed between clans in front of the wall and clans behind it, so she can't ride south to press her claim without abandoning the north.
   - The Regency and the March now have a dead company to blame each other for.
   - All of this chaos is exactly the kind a new emperor promises to end.
4. **Lysander answers.** Lysander sends a small company north under **Captain Aurel Dane**: about a dozen hired swords under a Regency sun banner, carrying a Quill writ. His goal is to retake the north bank and to be seen protecting the Ford-folk where their own duchess didn't. Lysander has no army to spare, and this is the best he can do cheaply. He doesn't tell Brenna. The orders reach Dane as *"retake the north bank."* Nobody in Solace knows a promise was made there.
5. **The river runs red.** Dane crosses at dawn. Varn meets him at his barricade with the clan-law challenge. Dane refuses it, and none of his company will yield. Varn kills them all, then sends **the sun banner down the river**.
6. **Varn crosses the line.** *"They cheated to make the promise, and they broke it anyway."* The warband crosses the river, the line Varn swore by. They burn **the village, the mill and the bridge from the south end**, which is how the adventure said it had to be done to destroy it for good. Varn doesn't harm anyone who flees or yields, so clan law still holds. Everything else burns.
7. **The refugees.**
   - **Edda** leads a column down the back lanes with the Millhaven carter. She still has her tally stick, and it has new notches. She ends up in **Millhaven**, at the Broken Antler.
   - **Most of the others** take the north road to **Solace** and camp outside the north gate. The Regent that sent Dane can see them from the palace.
   - **Fenn**, if he lived, goes north to the wall instead. Someone has to tell the towers.

### What it means

- **For the March:** the north road is cut at the river. The wall's winter bread now goes the long way round, on the back lanes and over fords nobody guards. The Ford-folk reeves, already tired, now have a burned village to point at.
- **For the clans:** the empire made a promise at the river and broke it again, exactly like Corwen. Varn went from the clan's most careful champion to proof the Cinders were right.
- **For Brenna:** her heroes gave away her north bank. A rider went to the Regent instead of to her. The Regent's men started a war in her March without asking. She is isolated and furious, and she blames Lysander first and the Lantern-sworn second.
- **For Lysander:** he lost a company and gained a refugee camp at his north gate. He will quietly blame Brenna's empty garrisons.
- **For the Heir:** a cheap win, and his best argument yet. *"The empire breaks every promise it makes to you. I keep mine."*

### Optional thread 3 pickup

Varn was waiting at the barricade the morning Dane crossed, because the Cinders knew the company's route. If you want the mole thread back, a surviving Ford-folk refugee can say so: *"The grey ones told him the day they'd come."* How did the Host know, when only the Order and the Regency did?

## Open questions

- Is a second allowed to cast at all? The duel rules say a second "can't strike," and a charm isn't a strike. The party found a gap in clan law. Decide whether the clans see it that way. *(The aftermath assumes Varn treats it as a southern trick but still keeps his word.)*
- What does the party hear, and when? The refugees in Millhaven and outside Solace are the natural way for the news to reach them.
- Does Edda blame the Lantern-sworn? She's an easy NPC to bring back angry, grateful, or both.
