# Calderra — Premise

> DM-only canon. Nothing in `world/` is shown on the player map unless it is copied into a public field.

Calderra is a continent in the first year after its empire fell, and every one-shot is a chance for the heroes to decide what rises in its place. Millhaven and its Fey Accord are one small village in one province: a local legend, not the main story.

## The premise

Six months ago, Emperor Castellan VII, called the Last Sun, died without an heir. On the night he died, the **Sunfire Crown** vanished from his brow. Its three gems are said to have survived, and scattered. The imperial legions went home, the roads went quiet, and every duke, guild and warlord with an army started calling themselves a rightful successor. People call this the **Age of Banners**.

## The clock

By imperial law, a new ruler is recognised at the **Council of Banners**, held in the capital one year after an emperor's death. But there is no Crown to give them. Whoever can bring the three gems together, and have a new crown forged, will shape what Calderra becomes. That gives the series its arc: a run of one-shots over one in-world year, then a finale.

**The big question for the table:** Should there be a new crown at all, and if so, whose head does it go on?

## The broken Crown: what it means

For three hundred years the Crown was the referee. Every charter, land grant, treaty, tax and contract in Calderra was sworn **to the Crown**, so everyone could trust the deal. The capital says everyone benefited. The provinces say they paid: Greenhollow's grain, the dwarves' taxes and the March's soldiers all flowed to Solace.

With the Crown gone and no true heir, it matters on three levels:

1. **Law (felt now).** Every oath sworn to the Crown is arguably void. That's why the Saltreach Compact could declare independence, why the dwarves stopped paying taxes, and why Ironhart's own grant to the March is suddenly open to argument. The Council of Banners can't crown anyone legitimate without a crown, and making one takes all three gems.
2. **Faith (felt by the people).** The Temple of the Unsetting Sun teaches the Sun took his crown with him, and there is no new dawn until a new crown is made. Every bad harvest and every raid reads as a sign.
3. **Magic (buried; see below).** Kept secret until late.

## The question at the heart of the series

**Go our own way, or put the empire back together, and under whom?** Every one-shot nudges Calderra toward many free peoples or one restored empire. The Council of Banners is where the players decide.

| Faction | Official answer | Inside tension |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stonecrown Holds | Independence | Deepkeepers want to seal the passes; Daylighters know they can't feed themselves alone. |
| House Ironhart | Reunify under Brenna; the wall needs an empire's treasury | Garrick and the Ford-folk want the March to look after itself. |
| The Regency | Keep the empire whole under a council | The Sunward Houses and the Temple want a crowned emperor. |
| Saltreach Compact | Independence (already declared) | Odessa's free captains want it for real; Hargrove's Old Houses secretly need the empire back. |
| The Ashen Pretender | Reunify by force, under him | The Host is a dozen peoples held together by one man; some chiefs would take land and go home. |

## What each faction has, and what it needs

When the emperor died, the empire stopped moving money around: no taxes in, no wall pay out. The money pooled wherever trade is. Each faction is rich in one thing and short of another, so they need each other.

| Faction | Rich in | Short of |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stonecrown Holds | Gold and iron | Food |
| House Ironhart | Soldiers and courage | Money |
| The Regency | Bread and legitimacy (the Granaries, the mint, the roads) | Money and soldiers |
| Saltreach Compact | Money and ships; banker of the war | Soldiers and land |
| The Ashen Pretender | Monsters and grievance | Legitimacy, until he proves his blood |

## Tone pillars

1. **Heroes in the gap.** The empire's protection is gone. Monsters raid roads, bandits rule bridges, and the people who step up are adventurers.
2. **Big, clear villains; grey politics.** The monsters and warlords are worth punching. The claimants are human: each has a fair point and a flaw.
3. **Every place is different.** Seven regions, seven flavours: siege warfare, sea chases, dragon hunts, courtly intrigue, village festivals.
4. **Choices stick.** Nothing resets. Who the heroes help this session shows up, grateful or angry, in a later one.

## Inspirations

The Lord of the Rings for heroic last stands, Game of Thrones (minus the cruelty) for rival claimants, Pirates of the Caribbean for the coast, The Princess Bride for swashbuckling banter, and Festival of the Accord for village-scale trials.

## Buried payoff: the Crown's third meaning (don't use for a while)

The Crown was a seal over three cracks to other worlds, and its gems each carry a gift and a cost. Its loss is why Fey Accords are fraying and strange things are leaking into the provinces. The full canon is in `sunfire-crown.md`. Drop hints now and then; reveal it late.
