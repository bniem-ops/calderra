# The Ashen Pretender

> **Status: draft.** Built fifth and last of the claimants, on purpose: his war is aimed at the weak points of every other faction. Items marked **(DM call)** are open choices.

**In one breath:** the Last Sun's real son, thrown away by the empire to hide its lies, who has built an army out of everyone else it threw away, and who is right about almost everything except what he's willing to burn to prove it.

## Origins and identity

**The story they tell (the Host's story).** The empire throws people away: orcs it walled out, sailors it sold, soldiers it branded, farmers it starved. Then the Sun threw away his own son. The Heir came back from the ash with nothing, and sat down to eat with the people nobody else would eat with. Now the thrown-away are coming home, and they're bringing the true emperor with them.

**How true it is.**
<!-- secret -->
Almost all of it.
- **His mother, Marit,** was the daughter of a Greenhollow innkeeper. About twenty-two years ago, on the Last Sun's final progress through the provinces, the emperor stayed at her father's inn. He gave her a ring when he left. Their son, **Rowan**, was born the next spring.
- **The exile.** When Rowan was about sixteen, in the third of the eight dark years, Marit took him and the ring to Solace to ask that he be recognised. Lysander exiled them to the far west and paid them to stay silent (see `regency.md`).
- **Marit's death.** She saved every coin of the hush money for her son's claim, and spent nothing on herself. The winter before the emperor died, she caught ash fever in the ruined fort where they lived, and refused to pay a healer out of "Rowan's gold." She died. Rowan was nineteen.
- **How he came to lead.** The ruined forts of the frontier were full of people the empire had thrown out: orc and goblin families whose ancestors fled west three hundred years ago when Corwen Ironhart broke the Thorn Pact, deserters, outlaws. Rowan grew up among them. He learned their tongues, ate at their fires, and buried his mother with their help. When the Last Sun died and the money stopped, he spent the hoard on their behalf: food first, then weapons, then hired ogres. The Host followed him because he was the first person with imperial blood who ever treated them as people.
<!-- /secret -->

**Names.**
- His followers call him **the Heir**.
- Everyone else calls him **the Ashen Pretender**.
- The Host calls itself **the Ashen Host**, or just "the Host." Its enemies call it **"the rabble"** and are learning not to.
- The frontier people call themselves **Ashfolk**.

**Defining trait.** They'd say: **nothing is wasted.** Not a person, not a coin, not a grudge. Outsiders would say: **they burn everything they touch.**

## Environment and survival

**What the Ashen Frontier gives:** ruined imperial forts (good walls, if you don't mind ghosts), old battlefields full of rusted weapons and better ones buried deeper, hardy goats, black-soil valleys where the old fires burned, and room. Nobody has wanted the frontier for a century.

**What it withholds:** almost everything else. The land was burned in the empire's first wars and never fully recovered. Little grows. The rivers run grey.

**How they feed themselves:** goats, ash-bread baked in the coals, roots, and what they raid. The Host eats from captured grain wagons. That is one reason the Greenhollow road is a target.

**Who does the hard labour:** everyone. The Heir carries water and digs latrines in front of his own soldiers. It's part of why they follow him.

**Biggest threat.** Themselves.
- The Host is a dozen peoples who mostly hate each other, held together by one man. If he dies, it splinters into raiders worse than the army.
<!-- secret -->
- And the man is dying. See *The big secret*.
<!-- /secret -->

## Power and structure

**Who decides.** The Heir, advised by **the Hearth**: Kestrel and the chiefs of every clan and company. They meet every night around one fire and eat from **one loaf**, torn by hand and passed around. A chief who won't eat from the loaf is no longer at the Hearth.

**How he got that right.** Blood, which he claims, and bread, which he gave. In the Host, the second counts for more.

**How power passes.** It doesn't. There is no successor, no council that could hold the Host without him, and no plan for after. This is the Host's greatest weakness, and Kestrel knows it.

**Who's at the bottom.** Prisoners and the newest recruits. Nobody stays at the bottom long: the Host promotes by deeds, fast.

**Breaking the rules.**
- **"Nothing is wasted."** Deserters aren't executed. They're sent to the front line of the next battle.
- **Stealing from the Host's stores:** you go without for as many days as you stole.
- **Harming a surrendered enemy:** out of the Host, unarmed, at dusk. The Heir is very strict about this one.

## Beliefs and values

The Host has no single faith: each clan keeps its own gods. What they share:

**Sacred:**
- **The loaf.** Bread shared by hand, from one loaf, at one fire. In a land the Granaries starved, breaking bread together is a vow.
- **Names.** Every member's name is spoken aloud at the Hearth on the night they join and the night they die. The empire forgot them. The Host doesn't.
- **The setting sun.** The upside-down sun on their banners: the empire's light has gone out, and they are what's left.

**Taboo:**
- **Building walls.** The Host lives in tents and camps in the open, even beside a ruined fort. A wall is what was used to keep them out.
- Eating alone when others are hungry.
- Wasting anything. A broken spear becomes two arrows.

**After death.** Each clan has its own belief. The Host's shared one: **you are remembered at the fire.** As long as your name is spoken at the Hearth, you are still in the Host. The Heir knows every name.

**Most praised virtue: loyalty.** To the Heir, to the Hearth, to whoever shares your bread.
**Tolerated vice: burning.** Fields, roads, granaries. "They taught us hunger. Now we teach it back."

**What they want from others:** land for the clans, a place for the thrown-away, recognition for the Heir. **What others want from them:** to be destroyed (most claimants), or to be used (Hargrove).

**What they misunderstand:**
- They think every faction is as rotten as the Regency. The March, the dwarves and Greenhollow have ordinary people who did nothing to them.

- They think the Heir will live long enough to rule. See below.

## Internal tension

**What they argue about.**
- **War or deal.** Some chiefs want land now and would take a treaty. Others want Solace burned.
- **The clans and the Branded.** The orc and goblin clans don't trust the human deserters, and the deserters don't trust the ogres.
- **The mask.** Many in the Host have never seen the Heir's face. Some think he's ashamed of his blood. Some think there's no one under it.

**Ideals vs. behaviour.** The Host says nothing is wasted and no one is thrown away. It burns grain roads that feed the poor, and sends deserters to die at the front.

**Who's unhappy, and what they'd do with power.**
- **Chief Grosk of the Red Hand**, the biggest orc clan in the Host, wants land in the March *now*, not after a crown. With power he'd take a treaty with Ironhart, or with anyone, and go home.
- **Kestrel**, the Heir's herald.
<!-- secret -->
Kestrel was the **Quill courier** who carried Lysander's hush money west every year. Sent to watch the exiles, Kestrel came to love them, and stayed. Kestrel knows the Regency's secrets: the Quiet Seal, the eight dark years, the stipend booked as wall pay. Lysander believes his courier died on the road. With power, Kestrel would keep the Heir alive at any cost, and is the only one who knows how much that cost is growing.
<!-- /secret -->

## Texture

**A typical day.** Drums at dawn. Every company drills, forages or marches. Raiding parties ride out; supply wagons ride in. At dusk, the Hearth: the chiefs eat from one loaf, and the names of the day's dead are spoken. Across the camp, every fire does the same with its own loaf.

**Celebration food:** whatever was taken from the enemy, shared out equally: grain-wagon bread, roast goat, captured wine. And always **ash-bread**, flat bread baked in the coals of the fire, black on the outside, eaten first by the youngest.

**Weddings.** The couple tears one loaf between them in front of the Hearth, and each eats the other's half.

**Funerals.** The name is spoken at the Hearth. The body is burned, and the ash is rubbed on the faces of the dead's company before the next battle.

**Sayings**
- "**Nothing is wasted.**"
- "Who threw you away?" (A greeting to a new recruit.)
- "They taught us hunger."

**Insults**
- "**Fence-builder**": anyone who walls others out.
- "Granary-fat."
- "Sun-blind": anyone who still believes in the empire.

**Superstitions**
- Never sleep inside walls if you can help it.
- If your name is forgotten at the Hearth, you'll die in the next fight. Say your own name twice before battle, to be sure.
- Never look under the Heir's mask. Some say it's bad luck. Some say worse.

## Story hooks

<!-- secret -->
**What they want right now.**
1. To reach the Council of Banners strong enough that no one can refuse the Heir.
2. **Proof.** Marit's ring proves little. The Last Sun's written acknowledgement of his son proves everything, and it is in Lysander's vault.
3. Land for the clans: the high pastures, by treaty with Ushra or by force.

**What's stopping them:** no legitimacy without proof; a Host that will splinter without him; and time.

### The big secret: the Will gem is burning him

- The night the Last Sun died, the Crown shattered. The **Will gem** didn't fall at the emperor's bedside. It appeared, still hot, on Marit's grave in the frontier, where Rowan found it at dawn.
- The Will gem gives its bearer an iron will, and gives that will to those who follow him: the Host fights without fear and marches without rest. It's a large part of why a dozen peoples who hate each other hold together.
- But it is **burning him from the inside.** His skin is turning to grey ash from the chest outward, slowly, like a fire banked in coals. The mask hides how far it has spread. That is why he is called *Ashen*, though no one outside the Hearth knows it.
- He has, at a guess, a year or two. He has to win before it finishes him. Kestrel knows, and is desperate.
- *(This is the first visible sign of the buried payoff: the gems are not only symbols. Use it to foreshadow the cracks to other worlds.)*

### The second secret: his face

The mask hides the ash-burn, and something else: **he has his father's face.** Anyone who saw the Last Sun young would know him at a glance. He is saving the unmasking for the Council of Banners, where it will be the proof no one can argue with.

### The third secret: the Greenhollow road

He burns the grain road to starve the dwarves and drive up the bread price in Solace. His mother grew up on that road.

**(DM call:** was Marit from **Millhaven**, and the younger sister of **MAX** the innkeeper? If so, MAX is the Pretender's uncle, the Heir has been sparing Millhaven while burning the rest of Greenhollow, and MAX's iron key from the Festival opens something of his sister's. It's a big link to the first session; it could also be too neat.)

### What would change or destroy them

- **Change:** proof that the empire can keep a promise. A treaty that gives the clans the high pastures and the Branded a pardon would take half his army off the field, and might make him choose peace.
- **Destroy:** killing the Heir (the Host splinters into a dozen warbands, which could be worse); the Will gem finishing its work; or Kestrel's past coming out, which would make the Host wonder what else the Heir doesn't know.

## Hooks for one-shots

- **Hold the Bridge at Harrow's Ford:** the raiders are an early Host company testing the March. A captured raider says, "The Heir doesn't want your bridge. He wants to see how long it takes Brenna to come."
- **The Regent's Vault:** the Host has its own team going after the acknowledgement on the same night. Two heists, one vault.
- **The Pretender's Mask:** the heroes witness the Hearth, the one loaf and the names. Whoever sees him unmasked sees the ash-burn and his father's face. The choice to depose him or turn him should now feel genuinely hard.
- **The Council of Banners:** the unmasking. Whether it ends the war or starts the last battle depends on what the heroes already did.

## Private hooks for Ashfolk PCs (hand out in person)

1. **The fort.** You grew up in the ruined fort where a woman from Greenhollow and her son lived. She taught you your letters. You were at her funeral.
2. **The courier.** Every winter a rider in Quill grey came west with a heavy saddlebag. One year the rider didn't leave. You know the rider's real name.
3. **The grave.** You were with the Heir on the morning he went to his mother's grave after the emperor died. He picked something up off it. He hasn't taken off his gloves since.
4. **The Branded.** You deserted from the Thornwall and joined the Host. You have a sister still on the wall. The Host's next target is her tower.

## For the Ashen Pretender

Recorded here instead: what the heroes could exploit in him.)* **His weakness is time and the Host itself.** Every week the gem spreads, and the Host only holds because he does. Slow the war down (hold the bridges, feed the Holds, keep bread cheap in Solace) and he runs out of time before he runs out of soldiers. Or offer the clans the treaty he can't, and the Host comes apart without a battle.
