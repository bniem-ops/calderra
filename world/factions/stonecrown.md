# Stonecrown Holds

> **Status: draft.** Built first: the dwarves were in the Ironspine before anyone else. Items marked **(DM call)** are open choices.

**In one breath:** the oldest people in Calderra, who live by the oath and the ledger, believe a name carved in stone is the only afterlife, and have spent forty years quietly cheating the empire out of dragon tribute for a dragon that was already dead.

## Origins and identity

**The story they tell.** The Ironspine dreamed, and its dream was the dwarves. The First Thane woke inside the mountain with a hammer already in her hand, and every clan descends from one of her seven sons. Dwarves did not *arrive* in Calderra; Calderra grew up around them.

**How true it is.** Half true. The dwarves are older than every lowland kingdom, but they came *up*, not out of the stone. Their oldest records, sealed in the Deep Archive, describe a great flight from lower halls after "the Deepfall", and the hasty sealing of a gate behind them: **the Sealed Door**. The mountain-dream story was written two generations later, so no one would ask what they fled. **(DM call:** what's behind the Door can stay unknown for a long time. It fits the buried payoff about cracks to other worlds if you want it to.)

**Names.**
- They call themselves **the Holdfast**, and the mountains simply **the Spine**. "Stonecrown" is the ruling clan, not the people.
- Lowlanders call them **Spiners**, or "the tax-dwarves" behind their backs.
- Imperial documents call them "the Ironspine Holds", which the dwarves find mildly insulting: iron is something you *dig*, not something you *are*.

**Defining trait.** They'd say: **we keep our word.** Outsiders would say: **they keep score.**

## Environment and survival

**What the Spine gives:** iron, silver, copper and good building stone; heat from the volcano's roots, which runs the great forges for free; deep mushroom farms, blind cave-fish, mountain goats, and spring water that never freezes.

**What it withholds:** grain, timber and anything green. The Holds have never fed themselves. Their bread comes from **Greenhollow**, bought with metal and hauled up the passes every autumn. This is the dwarves' quietest shame: a people who say they need no one depend on lowland farmers for every loaf.

**Who does the hard labour.** Every dwarf delves in their youth; it's a rite. But the deepest, hottest and most dangerous seams are worked by **the Oathless** (see below), and the surface terraces and caravans by hired lowlanders, who are paid well and never invited inside.

**Biggest threat.** Themselves. The Spine has never been conquered, but the Holds are rich, proud and shrinking: fewer children every generation, more gold in the vaults, and a grudge against every neighbour who might have helped. The dragon is the threat they talk about. Hunger is the one they don't.

## Power and structure

**Who decides.** Seven clans, seven seats at **the Moot**, which meets in the Hall of Names. The Moot elects a **Thane** for life, and the Thane rules day to day.

**How they got that right.** Each clan descends from one of the First Thane's sons, and the seats can't be bought or added. A clan that dies out leaves its seat empty and its stone chair turned to the wall. Two seats are empty now.

**How power passes.** In theory, any dwarf of any clan can be elected Thane. In practice, Clan Stonecrown has held the thaneship for **five generations**, because the Moot keeps electing the old Thane's chosen heir. Everyone pretends this is a coincidence.

**Who's at the bottom.** **The Oathless**: dwarves who broke a sworn oath. Their beards are shorn, their names chiselled off the Ledger Stone, and they work the deep seams for bread and a bunk. They put up with it because the only alternative is exile into the lowlands, which most dwarves consider worse than death. Some don't put up with it. See *Internal tension*.

**Breaking the rules.**
- A broken contract or debt: **repay it double**, publicly, at the Moot.
- A broken oath: **shorn and struck**, joining the Oathless. An Oathless can earn their name back with ten years of deep work, or one act the Moot judges great enough. Few do.
- Opening the Sealed Door or letting a hold-forge go cold through neglect: exile. No one living has seen it happen.

## Beliefs and values

**Sacred:** a sworn oath; the **Hall of Names**, where every honoured dwarf's name is carved; the forge-fire, which in the great holds has not gone out in a thousand years; a dwarf's **name-blade**, forged at their coming of age.

**Taboo:**
- Breaking an oath, even to a lowlander. *Especially* to a lowlander: it makes all dwarves liars.
- The Sealed Door: opening it, or speaking of it at the table.
- Selling a name-blade, or whistling below ground.

**After death.** A dwarf whose name is carved in the Hall of Names **is held by the mountain**, and remembered as long as stone lasts. A dwarf who dies uncarved simply **fades into the rock**. So the Holdfast care desperately about legacy, record-keeping and being remembered correctly. Erasing a name is the worst punishment they have. *(A nice echo of the Legacy Heroes list on the player map.)*

**Most praised virtue: endurance.** Keeping going, keeping your word, keeping the forge lit.
**Tolerated vice: the grudge.** A dwarf can hold a grudge for three hundred years and call it "a long memory." The Holds keep a **Grudge Ledger** next to the Hall of Names, and it is longer than the treaties.

## Relationships

| Group | Relationship | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Regency / the old empire | **Old grudge** | Centuries of imperial tax and little in return, or so the official story goes. |
| House Ironhart | **Rival, grudging respect** | Ironhart's Thornwall is built of dwarven stone, never fully paid for. The pass between the Spine and the March is disputed. Both sides think the other is greedy; both are proud. |
| Greenhollow | **Dependent, and they hate it** | The autumn grain caravans keep the Holds alive. Dwarves treat Greenhollow farmers with careful politeness and quiet condescension. |
| Saltreach Compact | **Best trading partner** | The Compact buys ore and sells everything else. They are the only lowlanders the dwarves respect, because they keep good books. |
| The red dragon of Kharag | **Old enemy, older bargain** | See *Secrets*. |
| Orc and giant clans | **Old enemies** | Raids on the northern passes; the dwarves remember every one, by name, in the Grudge Ledger. |

**What they want from others:** to be left alone, recognised as independent, and fed. **What others want from them:** iron, steel and stone; to be the anvil every claimant's army is forged on; and, for anyone who's guessed, a share of whatever's in their vaults.

**What they misunderstand:**
- They believe lowlanders will always come back begging, because they always have. They don't realise a hungry, war-torn lowland can't buy iron.
- They think Ironhart's pride is greed. It's duty. Brenna would hold the wall even if no one paid her.
- They think the empire never noticed the dragon fraud. **(DM call:** Regent Lysander noticed years ago and has been saving it for the right moment.)

## Internal tension

**What they argue about.**
- **The Deepkeepers vs. the Daylighters.** Traditionalists want to close the passes and outwait the Age of Banners. A younger faction wants to go down into the world: sell to every claimant, send a voice to the Council, and stop pretending the Holds don't need bread.
- **The empty seats.** Two clans are gone. Should the Oathless be allowed to found a new clan and fill a seat? Saying yes would admit the Holds are dying.

**Ideals vs. behaviour.** They are the people who never break their word, and for forty years they've collected dragon tribute for a dead dragon (see *Secrets*). They justify it the way grudge-keepers always do: the empire owed them.

**Who's unhappy, and what they'd do with power.**
- **Hilde Ashforge**, Durgan's niece and his likely successor, leads the Daylighters. Brilliant, impatient, and the first Stonecrown in years to want the Moot to actually choose. With power she'd open the Holds to trade, back a claimant (probably Ironhart), and **open the Sealed Door** for the riches the old records describe.
- **Torvek Nameless**, spokesman of the Oathless, once a master smith. He knows about the tribute fraud; he was struck for refusing to forge the "tribute seals." With power he'd expose it, and the Oathless would gladly follow a lowland claimant who promised to restore their names.

## Texture

**A typical day.** The holds run on **bell-shifts**: three eight-hour turns marked by the great bell, because there's no sun below. An ordinary Holdfast wakes to the bell, eats black bread and mushroom porridge, works a shift at the forge, the mine or the ledger-house, then spends the evening in the clan hall: ale, songs that list names, and arguments about who owes whom.

**Celebration food:** **forge-bread** baked directly on the anvil stones, goat roasted over volcanic vents, black mushroom stew, cave-fish smoked with juniper, and **Kharag Dark**, a stout so thick it's poured with a spoon. Lowland fruit is the true luxury: a wedding with apples on the table is a rich wedding.

**Weddings.** The couple's names are carved together on one stone, then each pledges an oath to the other's clan. The marriage is sealed when they forge a single ring together, one striking and one holding.

**Funerals.** The body is laid in the stone of the deep halls. The clan gathers in the Hall of Names while the eldest carver cuts the name. The last line of the funeral song is the name itself, sung once by everyone.

**Sayings**
- "**Stone remembers.**" (A promise, a threat and a blessing, depending on tone.)
- "Measure twice, swear once."
- "Bread is borrowed; iron is owed."

**Insults**
- "**Paper-promise**": a lowlander, or anyone whose word is worth as little as the parchment.
- "May your name wear smooth."
- "Soft-handed."

**Superstitions**
- Never whistle below ground; it calls something up from under the Sealed Door.
- A forge that sputters means someone in the clan has told a lie.
- Never say the old dragon's name inside the mountain. (This one has a practical reason; see below.)

## Story hooks

**What they want right now.**
1. Independence recognised at the Council of Banners.
2. A guaranteed grain supply through the war.
3. The dragon problem solved quietly, before anyone asks what happened to the old one.

**What's stopping them:** their own grudges (they won't ask anyone for help), the grain road through a war zone, and a dragon they can't control.

### The big secret: the dead dragon

- The old red dragon **Kharagax**, the one the mountain is named for, died **forty years ago** of age, in her sleep, deep in the volcano.
- The Holds found her body, told no one, and **kept collecting the imperial tribute** sent every year to keep the dragon asleep. The "tribute seals" proving delivery were forged by their best smiths. Forty years of imperial gold sits in the Stonecrown vaults.
- But Kharagax left **an egg in the volcano's heart**. It hatched about twenty years ago. The dwarves have been feeding the young dragon to keep it docile, and telling themselves it would stay small.
- When the emperor died, the tribute stopped, and the dwarves stopped feeding it to save their stores. **The smoke over Mount Kharag is the hungry young dragon, not the old one waking.**
- This is why the Dragon of Mount Kharag is a level 9 fight with a *young* red dragon, and why Thane Durgan is so eager for heroes to "renegotiate."

**The second secret:** what lies behind the Sealed Door. Leave it locked for now.

### What would change or destroy them

- **Change:** a lowland ally who feeds them through the war without asking for anything in return, or the Moot actually electing someone outside Clan Stonecrown.
- **Destroy:** the tribute fraud made public (every claimant would demand the gold back, and the Holds' word, their whole identity, would be worthless), the grain road cut for a winter, or the Sealed Door opened.

## Hooks for one-shots

- **The Dragon of Mount Kharag:** add the dead-dragon secret as the twist. A party that finds Kharagax's bones learns the truth and has to decide whether to expose it.
- **The Millhaven Harvest:** one of the two armies wanting Millhaven's grain could be a Holdfast quartermaster with a full purse, not soldiers.
- **The Feast of Banners:** Hilde Ashforge comes to the feast instead of Durgan, and wants a private word with Ironhart.
- **Torvek Nameless** makes a good informant in any Act II one-shot: he wants his name back and knows where the forged seals were made.

## For the Ashen Pretender

**The weakness he'd exploit: bread.** The Holds can't feed themselves and won't ask for help. The Pretender doesn't need to assault the Spine; he only needs to burn the Greenhollow road and wait for winter. Then he offers grain, and his price is the gold in the vaults, the Oathless as his smiths, and the Holds' silence.
