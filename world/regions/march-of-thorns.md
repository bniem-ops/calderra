# The March of Thorns

Setting for The Line at Harrow's Ford.

This is a **regional pass**. The Marchers, House Ironhart and their secrets are in `factions/ironhart.md`; how the March fits with the other regions is in `world/geography.md`. This file covers the ground.

## The land

The March is a long, cold strip across the top of Calderra. Its northern edge is the **Thornwall**, which runs from the sea in the west to the foot of the Ironspine in the east. South of the wall lie the open, frozen grasslands of the north, sheep and horse country, where steadings sit a day apart. Then comes the river, with **Harrow's Ford** on its last bridge. South of the river, the land softens into the Ford-folk's rye fields and river villages before it runs down toward the Heartlands.

Everything in the March faces north. Towers, doors and graves all point that way, and even the Ford-folk build their houses with the windows on the north wall.

**What's changed since the emperor died:**
- **The wall pay stopped.** The imperial treasury paid the Thornwall's garrison for three hundred years. The money stopped the day the emperor died, and the March has been holding the wall out of its own pocket since.
- **The garrisons went north.** Every soldier the March can spare is on the wall. The bridges and villages behind it are guarded by reeves, old men and whoever is home on leave.
- **The duchess is going south.** Brenna is claiming the throne, and the north is watching her go.
- **The clans came by sea.** For three centuries the wall was enough because the clans had no ships. This year, a warband landed behind it (see *The Line at Harrow's Ford*). Every Marcher now looks over their shoulder as well as north.
- **Sun-marks stay.** Marchers keep the imperial suns on their tower gates scrubbed and bright. The wall was sworn to the Crown, and a Marcher doesn't leave a post just because nobody is checking.

## Places a party passes through

| Place | What it is | What a party finds there |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Thornwall** | The wall itself: dwarven stone, twelve towers, a walkway along the top, and a beacon on every tower. It runs from the sea to the mountains. | Wall-year soldiers aged sixteen to nineteen, old wardens, the wallbound hauling stone, and the beacon chain lit every night. |
| **The High Tower** | The tallest tower, at the **Thorn Gap**, where Corwen held against the giants. Garrick Ironhart is its warden. | The best-defended spot in the March, and Garrick's letters. |
| **The Sea Tower** | The wall's western end, built out onto the rocks where it meets the sea. | The tower that should have seen the warband's boats. It faces north, and they came from behind. |
| **Ironhart Keep** | The ducal seat, a grey stone keep a day south of the High Tower, with the family chapel. | The Thorn Council's hall, the chapel curtain, and Brenna's household packing to go south. |
| **The steadings** | Sheep and horse farms scattered across the grassland between the wall and the river. | Families who stay put through the winter, and empty farms where the raid came through. |
| **Harrow's Ford** | The last bridge before the wall, with a mill and a village behind a low fence. | Reeve Edda, the bridge and its seven stone piers, and whatever the heroes left behind. |
| **The Ford villages** | Rye-farming villages along the river south of the Ford. | The Ford-folk reeves, the March's tax-payers, and its first victims. They're tired. |
| **The high pass** | The pass from the March's eastern end into the Ironspine, claimed by both the March and the Holds. | Two toll-posts a mile apart, one dwarven and one Marcher, that don't speak to each other. Closed in winter. |
| **The wall's foot** | The ground right below the Thornwall: pine woods, frostberry thickets, and the stones clan children throw back. | Wallbound work gangs, berry-pickers, and the best place in the March for a quiet word with someone from the north. |

**Roads:** the **north road** runs from Solace through Harrow's Ford to the High Tower. A track runs along the wall's inside from tower to tower. Back lanes run south to Greenhollow, used by the grain carters. The high pass leads east into the Ironspine. Travel times are in `world/geography.md`.

## The March in its adventure

- **The Line at Harrow's Ford (Act I):** the bridge, the river, the refugees from the steadings and the warband that came around the wall. The adventure file has the full map key. Its ripples change the March for every later visit: whether the bridge stands, whether the refugees went south to Greenhollow, and whether Varn went home.
- **Later visits:** at Act II levels (6–9), a March one-shot should be on the wall itself. At Act III levels (11–13), it should be beyond it, in the clan lands.

## Secrets of the ground

- **The hollow towers.** Three of the twelve towers are empty, kept lit by two old wardens on a circuit, with straw wardens on the walkway. On the ground, a party passing one at night sees a lit beacon and helmeted figures who never move, and in the morning no smoke from the barracks chimney. See `ironhart.md`.
- **The clan lands aren't empty.** The ground north of the wall has old clan cairns, terraced high pastures gone to grass, and stone markers carved in the clans' script along the line of the Thorn Pact. Anyone who goes north and can read them learns the land once had a border further south.
- **The Sea Tower is the weak end.** The wall stops at the sea, and the clans now have boats. The Sea Tower's warden has asked for a second beacon facing south. The Thorn Council said no, because it would tell everyone the wall can be bypassed.
- **Garrick's crossroads.** Garrick's courier meets the Pretender's herald at a crossroads stone on the north road, halfway between the Keep and Harrow's Ford, marked by a carved thorn. A party that watches it long enough sees a grey mask.
- **Blue beacons (late-series hint).** From Act II, beacons begin burning blue on towers where nobody has died. Marchers say the dead are restless. See `world/geography.md`.

## Hooks for one-shots

- **The hollow tower (filler, Act II):** the heroes are sent to relieve a tower's garrison and find straw. Then the beacon down the line goes dark.
- **The wallbound (filler, Act I–II):** a gang of convicts escapes during a raid, and one of them knows something the duchess would pay to keep quiet.
- **The high pass (filler, Act II):** the dwarven and Marcher toll-posts are about to come to blows over a grain caravan. The heroes are the only neutral party on the mountain.
- **North of the wall (Act III):** the clans' cairns, Ushra's camp, and the Thorn Pact's border stones. A diplomatic one-shot at high level, where a fight is the failure state.

## Open questions for the DM

- The rest of the twelve towers are unnamed. Name them when a session needs them, or now?
