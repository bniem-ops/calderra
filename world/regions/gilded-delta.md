# The Gilded Delta

No one-shot is set here yet. Vareth reaches into The Black Gull (Option A: a Vareth prince funds the piracy) and into every claimant's purse.

Vareth has no faction file, so this file does the job of one at regional depth, the same as `greenhollow.md`: the land, the people, how they're governed, what everyone wants from them, and their secrets. A full faction pass (and a player guide) can build on it later. How the Delta fits with the other regions is in `world/geography.md`.

**Names to remember (four):** Prince Aurelio Vey (the Pearl), Prince Teodor Lusk (see the secrets), the Golden Chain, and the Gilded Companies.

**In one breath:** the richest city in Calderra, which grows half its bread and owns the capital's only road to the sea, run by bought princes who never settle a debt because a debt is a hold on someone, and whose vaults hold a third of the gold their paper promises.

## The land

The Sunwater leaves the Heartlands broad and slow and runs south-east for four days by barge. Where the land flattens near the sea, it splits around the islands of Vareth into seven mouths that spread across a great fan of silt, reeds and mangrove.

- **Upriver: the polders.** Fields walled against the river with earth dykes, growing wheat and rice. This is Calderra's other breadbasket. Greenhollow grows more; the Delta sells smarter.
- **On the levees: orange groves and mulberries.** Delta oranges are the luxury on every wedding table in Calderra. The mulberry farms feed the silkworms behind Vareth's silk.
- **In the middle: Vareth.** A city of islands, bridges and canals, built on oak piles driven into the mud. Painted palaces, gilded domes, and barges where carts would be.
- **At the edge: the mangrove.** The outer Delta is a maze of channels, reeds and fog, full of birds, eels, smugglers and things that bite. Only one of the seven mouths is deep enough for a seagoing ship, and the Golden Chain stretches across it.

**What it gives:** grain, rice, oranges, silk, fish and eels, and above all **position**: everything that travels by water between Solace and the sea passes through Vareth.

**What it withholds:** stone, timber and iron, all bought in; firm ground (Vareth is always sinking, a finger's width a generation); and a healthy summer (fevers come up from the mangrove).

**Who does the hard labour:** polder-workers and rowers, many of them from **the Pawn**. They work off family debts that grow faster than they can be paid. A Reacher bond lasts five years. A Vareth debt lasts three generations.

**Biggest threat:** themselves, and the river. The Delta is a city of paper built on mud. If enough people demanded gold for their notes at once, or the dykes failed, it would sink either way.

**Roads:** in the Delta, water is the road. Barges run up the Sunwater to Solace; ships leave through the Chain for the southern lanes, toward the Reach and the islands; flat-bottomed boats work the canals and the outer channels. A poor road runs north along the river to the Heartlands, used by people who can't afford a barge. Travel times are in `world/geography.md`.

## Origins and identity

**The story they tell:** Vareth was here first, a city of sea-kings at the river mouth, and the empire never conquered it. The first emperor came down the river with an army, saw the city, and bought it instead. Vareth has been buying things ever since.

**How true it is:** half true. Vareth was an old trading town, and it did surrender without a fight, in return for a charter to run the river toll. The princes are not sea-kings' heirs. A century ago, in a bad year, the imperial treasury sold titles to raise money, and Vareth's twelve richest families bought every princedom on offer. The Solari still call them **"paper princes."**

**Names:**
- They call themselves **Varethi**, and the Delta-folk upriver call themselves **dyke-folk**.
- Reachers call the princes **"paper princes"** or "fog-bankers." The dwarves call Delta notes "paper-promises", their worst insult.
- Varethi call Reachers **"fishwives with ledgers."**

**What defines them, they'd say:** *grace.* Doing anything, even losing, beautifully. **Outsiders would say:** everyone in Vareth owes everyone else, and they call it society.

## Power and structure

- **The Princes' Court** of twelve houses governs Vareth. Each prince holds a bank, a canal ward and a vote.
- **The Pearl** presides for a year. By custom it is whichever prince is **owed the most** on the last night of the year. In Vareth, power is being owed. The current Pearl, **Prince Aurelio Vey**, is owed money by half the claimants in Calderra.
- **The Exchange** is where power really moves. A prince who buys up another's debts can call them in all at once, and houses have fallen in a night that way.
- **Who's at the bottom:** the Pawn, the polder-workers and the rowers. They put up with it because Vareth always has work, and because once in a generation a debtor wins a duel as a hired champion, clears a family's account and buys a house on the Grand Canal. Everyone in the Pawn knows that story.
- **Breaking the rules:**
  - **Default:** your debts are called, all at once, by everyone. Your house is sold, and your name goes into the Pawn.
  - **An insult:** settled on the Dawn Steps at sunrise, by you or a champion you hire. Refusing a duel is worse than losing one.
  - **Cheating at a duel:** the cheat's house is barred from the Exchange for a year, which usually means ruin.

## Beliefs and values

- **Sacred:** the open account (a debt between friends is never settled, because settling it would end the friendship); face (how things look *is* how things are); and the river, which made the city and will one day take it.
- **Taboo:** settling a debt in full with a friend (it's an insult: "a settled account is a closed door"); showing poverty in public; rowing the eighth mouth.
- **After death:** at the funeral, the dead's ledger is read aloud, with every debt and every credit, and passed to the heirs. Then the body goes downriver at night on a gilded barge, burning. The bigger the ledger, the bigger the barge.
- **Most praised virtue:** grace under pressure. A good duellist, a good host and a good loser are the same person.
- **Tolerated vice:** debt, which they don't consider a vice at all. Bribery, which they call *courtesy*.
- **The Temple:** Vareth funds the richest Temple shrine outside Solace, on the main island, and attends it for show. The princes have read the High Prelate's sermon that only a crown on a blood heir brings a new dawn, and they've done the sums on what that blood heir would be worth.

**What they want from others:** to be owed, by everyone, and to be at the table when the Council of Banners decides who pays for the next empire. **What others want from them:** loans, soldiers, grain, and passage through the Chain.

**What they misunderstand:**
- They think everything is for sale, the same mistake the Reach makes, in silk.
- They think a debt is power. It's only power while the debtor can pay.
- They think the war is a market. They think they'll be the ones holding the gold when it closes.

## The Delta this year

Vareth has never been richer. Every claimant needs soldiers, grain and money, and Vareth has all three.

- **The Banner Auctions.** Each month at the Exchange, contracts for the Gilded Companies go to the highest bidder. Every claimant's agent attends. So does one nobody can name.
- **Loans to everyone.** Lady Corisande, the Sunward Houses, Reacher captains short of hulls, and Greenhollow parishes short of seed have all borrowed from Vareth this year.
- **The toll goes up.** The Golden Chain's toll has doubled since the emperor died. Solace pays it in the Morning Price.
- **The gem offer.** Prince Aurelio Vey has publicly offered a palace on the Grand Canal for any Crown gem brought to him. He doesn't want to wear a crown. He wants to auction a third of one.

**What the Princes' Court is arguing about:** whether to back one claimant (and own the winner) or keep selling to all of them (and own the war). Aurelio wants to keep selling. **Prince Teodor Lusk** wants to pick a side early and be paid in land. The other ten follow whoever is winning at the Exchange that week.

**Who's unhappy:** the Pawn, whose debts grow with every price rise; the dyke-wardens, who say the dykes need repairs the princes won't pay for; and the Gilded Companies' old legates, who took Vareth's coin and miss serving something bigger.

## Texture

**A typical day:** a bell from the Exchange at dawn, the day's prices chalked on the great board, and the duels at the Dawn Steps. The princes do business over long lunches and longer dinners. Barges move all day; gondolas all night. A night in Vareth is lamplit, loud and expensive.

**Celebration food:** eels in orange sauce, saffron rice with river prawns, honeyed orange peel, sugared almonds, black rice wine, and imported everything. At a prince's table, the luxury is to serve something everyone else can't get: dwarven cheese, Greenhollow apples or snow from the Ironspine.

**A wedding:** both families' ledgers are opened, and each spouse takes on the other's debts in front of witnesses. The couple then crosses the Grand Canal in one boat, rowed by the guests.

**A funeral:** the reading of the ledger, then the burning barge downriver at night. The heirs stand on the bank and don't look away until the light is gone.

**Sayings:**
- "A settled account is a closed door."
- "Pay the toll." (Accept the cost of doing business; also, don't complain.)
- "Grace first, gold second." (Said by people who have gold.)

**Insults:** "Settled" (someone nobody owes anything, so they matter to no one); "fishwife"; "mud-born."

**Superstitions:** never row the eighth mouth; never pay a debt on a duel morning; a gondola with no lamp at night is carrying someone's death.

## Secrets

- **The hollow vaults.** Vareth's banks issue notes promising gold on demand. The vaults hold about a third of what the notes promise. The Gilded Companies are paid in notes. The whole Delta runs on everyone not asking at once. If someone with a grudge and a lot of notes (the Old Houses of Saltreach, or the dwarves) called them all in, Vareth would fall like a house on the Exchange. See *The hollow count* in `world/geography.md`.
- **Teodor Lusk and the Black Gull.** Prince Teodor Lusk pays Captain Jory Vane to raid the southern lanes. The piracy drives up freight prices, the Reach's ships lose money, and Delta grain sells dearer. This is Option A in The Black Gull. In Option B, Vane takes Teodor's money and chooses his own targets anyway. See `adventures/02-black-gull/adventure.md`.
- **Corisande's note.** Lady Corisande paid the poisoner at the Feast of Banners with a loan from Prince Aurelio Vey. Aurelio holds her note. If her son Leo is crowned, the Pearl of Vareth owns the throne's mother. Aurelio doesn't know what the money was for, and doesn't want to.
- **The bidder at the auctions.** The unnamed buyer at the Banner Auctions is a Host agent, paying in old imperial gold from the Heir's hoard. The Exchange knows the coin is imperial and has decided not to ask.
- **The legates' price.** The commander of the largest Gilded Company is an old legate who served the Last Sun. She takes Vareth's coin, but she still carries the legion's standard. For a crown that was really forged, with all three gems, she'd fight for nothing.
- **The eighth mouth (late-series hint).** In fog, the Sunwater has eight mouths, not seven. Boats that take the eighth come back a day late, or not at all. The Delta isn't on one of the three true cracks; this is the world thinning as the seals fail. See `world/geography.md`.

### What would change or destroy them

- **Change:** someone calling Vareth's notes and Vareth surviving it, which would mean the princes finally keeping gold behind their word.
- **Destroy:** a run on the banks; the dykes failing; or a Council of Banners that cancels every debt sworn to the Crown, which would void the Chain's charter along with the Sun Bonds.

## Hooks for one-shots

- **The Dawn Steps (filler, Act I–II):** a prince hires the heroes as duel champions. Each duel at dawn settles a different quarrel, and by the third the heroes realise the quarrels are all about the same thing.
- **The Banner Auction (filler, Act II):** guard the auction, rig it or rob it. Every claimant is bidding, and one bidder pays in coin with the old emperor's face on it.
- **The gem palace (filler, Act II):** someone has brought Aurelio a Crown gem, and it's a fake. The forger has left with the deed to a palace, and the Pearl wants it back quietly.
- **The run (filler, Act II–III):** someone starts calling Vareth's notes. The heroes are hired to find out who, by people who would very much prefer the answer was "nobody."
- **The Golden Chain (filler, Act III):** in the Council's last weeks, someone wants the Chain closed so Solace can't be supplied by water. The heroes are on the barge that has to get through.

## For the Ashen Pretender

**The weakness he'd exploit: everything is for sale.** He doesn't need to fight Vareth. He bids at its auctions with the empire's own gold, buys the Gilded Companies away from whoever hired them last, and lets the princes believe they're profiting from the war. When it suits him, he'll tell the Reach and the Holds how little gold is in Vareth's vaults, and watch the city call its own debts.

## Open questions for the DM

- Everything here is new canon except the Black Gull link, the bank rivalry, the oranges, the grain and the gem rumour. The biggest calls to check: power going to whoever is **owed** the most; the **Golden Chain** giving Vareth control of Solace's route to the sea; the **hollow vaults**; and **Corisande's loan** tying Vareth to the Feast of Banners.
- Teodor Lusk is now the prince behind Option A of The Black Gull. Happy with a named villain there, or keep him anonymous?
- Is Vareth worth a **player guide** (*Playing a Varethi*) and a full faction pass, or is this enough for an off-stage power?
- Does the Delta need a one-shot of its own? The Banner Auction or the Dawn Steps would fit Act II, and the Delta is the only region without an adventure.
