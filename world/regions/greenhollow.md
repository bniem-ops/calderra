# Greenhollow Shires

Home of Millhaven and MAX. Quietly one of the most important places in Calderra. Settings: Festival of the Accord, The Millhaven Harvest.

Greenhollow has no faction of its own, so this file does the job a faction file does elsewhere: the land, its people, how they govern themselves, what everyone else wants from them, and what they hide. The Verdant Court (Millhaven's Fey) still needs its own pass; it's sketched here only as far as the Shires see it.

## Who's who (quick reference)

| **The Hollow** | The old wood on the Fey border near Millhaven, where the Accord was sworn. Nobody goes in after dark. |

**Names to remember (five):** MAX, Elder Aldric of Millhaven, Factor Osric Pell at the Long Barns, the Shire-moot, and the Hollow.

## The land

The Shires sit west of the capital: hedged fields, slow rivers, orchards, mills and small stone villages a half-day's walk apart. To the west the farmland thins into scrub and burned forts (the Ashen Frontier); to the north is the March; to the north-east the passes climb into the Ironspine; to the east the grain road runs straight to Solace.

**What it gives:** grain above all (wheat, rye, barley), plus apples, cider, wool, cheese, honey and good timber from the hedgerow oaks. In a normal year Greenhollow grows far more than it eats. That surplus feeds Solace, the dwarves, the March in winter and half the coast.

**What it withholds:** defences. The Shires are flat, open and full of roads. There are no walls worth the name, no castles, and no army. For three hundred years the empire's legions were the walls. Now there is nothing between the farms and anyone who wants them.

**How they feed themselves:** easily, which is the whole problem. The hard labour is done by the farming families themselves, by hired harvest hands who walk the Shires from field to field, and, since this winter, by refugees from the north (see *The Line at Harrow's Ford*).

**The biggest threat:** neighbours. Every army in Calderra needs bread, and Greenhollow is the bread.

### Places a party passes through

- **Millhaven.** The Festival village. Lanterns strung between the houses, the round stone coliseum on the green, the Broken Antler (the largest building in town). Since the Festival, a lamp burns in many windows for the Lantern-sworn.
- **Wendle Cross.** A busy market town where the grain road, the pass road and the north road meet. The **Long Barns**, a row of stone granaries with the imperial sun painted over each door, take in the grain tax. Inns, a horse fair, a Temple shrine, and lately a lot of men in other people's colours.
- **The grain road.** Wide, paved, milestoned, with a coaching inn every day's ride. It runs from Wendle Cross to Solace, about five days by cart. Since spring, its western end has started burning.
- **The pass road.** Narrower, climbing north-east through hill villages to the Ironspine. In autumn it fills with dwarven wagons, Holdfast caravan guards and haggling.
- **Hedgeend.** The last parish before the Frontier. Burned out in the spring. Its people are in Millhaven and Wendle Cross, sleeping in barns.
- **The Hollow.** An old, dark wood east of Millhaven where the hedges stop. The Accord was sworn at a standing stone inside it. Paths there go where they want to.
- **Travel times:** Millhaven to Wendle Cross is a day by cart; Wendle Cross to Solace five; Wendle Cross to the first Ironspine hold about six, uphill; Millhaven to Harrow's Ford about four, north.

## Origins and identity

**The story they tell:** the Shires were here before the empire and will be here after it. The first farmers cleared the land, planted the hedges and made a bargain with the Fey of the Hollow: the farmers keep their side, the Fey keep theirs, and the harvest comes in. The empire was just another thing that came along wanting some of the harvest.

**How true it is:** mostly true. The Shires are old, and the hedge-parishes predate the empire. The bit they leave out is that they did very well out of it: imperial roads carried their grain to markets they could never have reached alone, and imperial legions kept the Frontier off their fields.

**What defines them, they'd say:** *keeping your bargain.* The Accord, the grain tax, the harvest hire, the price agreed at the fair: a Shire-folk handshake is good. **Outsiders would say:** slow, stubborn, and not interested in anything beyond the next hedge.

## Power and structure

- **Parish councils** run each village: the heads of the old farming families, plus an elder chosen by them (Millhaven's is **Aldric**). They settle boundaries, hire watchmen, set the Festival, and decide who pays to mend the bridge.
- **Reeves** collected the grain tax for the empire and still hold the parish tally-sticks. They were the empire's men in the Shires and now don't know who they work for.
- **The Shire-moot** brings every council together. By custom it's called only when the whole of Greenhollow is in danger. It was last called during a blight ninety years ago. This year it has been called for the first time in living memory, to answer one question: **now the Crown is gone, who do we sell to?**
- **Who's at the bottom:** harvest hands, who own nothing and walk from farm to farm; and now the refugees from the north and from Hedgeend. They put up with it because there's work and bread. That holds until the bread runs short.
- **Breaking the rules:** a bargain-breaker is "put outside the hedge." Nobody will trade with them, hire them or sit with them at the Festival. It's a quiet punishment and a total one.

## Beliefs and values

- **Sacred:** the bargain. The Accord most of all, but any honest deal. Also the first sheaf of every harvest, which is left standing in the field "for the Hollow."
- **Taboo:** breaking your word; eating or drinking anything offered in the Hollow; cutting a hedge you didn't plant.
- **After death:** the dead are buried at the field edge, under the hedge, so they keep watching over the land. "Gone under the hedge" means dead.
- **Most praised virtue:** steadiness. Turning up, every season, and doing the work.
- **Tolerated vice:** keeping your head down. The Shires let the world's trouble go past their gate if it'll go. That has worked for three hundred years.
- **The Temple:** there's a Sun shrine in every market town, and Shire-folk are polite to it. Their real faith is the hedge and the harvest. The Temple's teaching that there's no new dawn without a new crown has landed hard here this year, because the harvest is a sign everyone can read.

## Relationships

| Group | How the Shires see them | How it actually stands |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Regency** | The empire's buyer, who paid little and sold the bread back dear | Solace depends on Shire grain and barely knows where Greenhollow is. The Granary factor at the Long Barns is now demanding the tax with no legions behind him. |
| **House Ironhart** | Soldiers up north, gruff, fine to sell rye to | The March has guarded Greenhollow's flank for a century, partly paid for by Greenhollow's own grain tax. Almost no farmer knows. |
| **Stonecrown Holds** | Good customers who pay in silver and never say thank you | The Holds can't survive a winter without the pass-road caravans. They'd never admit it. |
| **Saltreach Compact** | Fair dealers who pay better than the Granaries | Reacher buyers are all over the Shires this year, offering the best price anyone has seen. |
| **The Ashen Pretender** | Raiders from the burned west who torch barns and roads | His Host burns the western grain road to starve the dwarves and squeeze Solace. |
| **The Order of the Dawn Lantern** | The Lantern-sworn who saved the Festival. Heroes. | Lamps in windows across the Shires. The Order is the one outside power Greenhollow trusts. |
| **The Verdant Court** | Dangerous neighbours with whom we have a deal | The Accord has held for decades. It's fraying. |

**What they want from others:** to be left alone, and to be paid properly. **What others want from them:** grain, at their price, now.

**What they misunderstand:**
- They think the empire's grain tax only fed Solace. Some of it built the Thornwall.
- They think they can sell to everyone and stay out of the war. Every army that buys grain is now a side they've fed.
- They think the Accord is a local custom. It's part of something much larger.

## The Shires this year

For the first time in three centuries, nobody can tell Greenhollow who to sell to. The imperial grain tax was sworn to the Crown; with the Crown gone, every reeve's tally is arguably void. That makes this the richest and most dangerous harvest the Shires have ever had.

**What the Shire-moot is arguing about:**
- **Keep the old contracts.** Sell to the Long Barns as always. The old families and the reeves want this; it's familiar, and Solace will starve otherwise. (Factor Pell has been visiting these families.)
- **Sell to the highest bidder.** The Reacher buyers pay twice what the Granaries do; the dwarves pay in silver. The younger farmers want this. It would make Greenhollow rich, and every claimant who loses out angry.
- **Close the hedges.** Keep the grain, store it, sell nothing until the war's over and the price is right. Popular with people who've seen Hedgeend burn. It would starve Solace and the Holds by midwinter.
- **Stand together.** A few voices, MAX among them, say the Shires should speak as one, choose their own terms and send someone to the Council of Banners. Nobody's sure the Shires have ever been one thing.

**Who's unhappy:** the harvest hands and refugees, who'd lose most if the barns close; the reeves, whose authority vanished with the Crown; and the young, who want to fight back against the Host and have nobody to lead them.

## Texture

**A typical day:** up before dawn; animals first, then the fields; bread and cheese at noon under a hedge; the inn at dusk for cider and news. This year the news is mostly about armies, and about the children's dreams.

**Celebration food:** the **harvest loaf** (a great plaited loaf shaped like a sheaf, broken by the eldest at the table), apple and pork pie, honey cakes, hard cheese, new cider and old perry. At the Festival, roast meat over open fires on the green.

**A wedding:** the couple plant a hedge sapling together at the boundary between their families' fields, and swear their vows as a bargain, with witnesses. Shire-folk say a marriage is the only bargain you renew every morning.

**A funeral:** the body goes under the hedge at the field edge, wrapped in wool. Everyone throws in a handful of their own field's earth. Then a long, loud wake at the inn.

**Sayings:**
- "A bargain kept is a harvest in." (Do what you said and things work out.)
- "Gone under the hedge." (Dead.)
- "Mind the first sheaf." (Don't be greedy; leave the Fey their share.)
- "Outside the hedge." (Shunned; or simply foreign. Both are bad.)
- "Rain comes for kings and carters alike."

**Superstitions:** never thank a stranger in the Hollow (it counts as a bargain); never take the last apple off a tree; a lamp left burning in a window keeps away whatever walks the roads at night (an old custom, newly popular since the Lantern-sworn).

## Story hooks

**What the Shires want right now:** to get this harvest in and sold without being burned, robbed or dragged into a war. **What's stopping them:** every army in Calderra needs their grain before winter, and they have no one to defend them.

### The secrets

- **The Mind gem is under Millhaven.** It lies in the dead roots of the Festival's Challenger, beneath the coliseum, and it's leaking. Millhaven's children (and now children in nearby parishes) all dream the same dream: a green light, a door in a hedge, someone on the other side. See `world/sunfire-crown.md`.
- **The Hollow is a crack.** The Accord wasn't only a deal with the Fey. It was a local patch over the same crack the Mind gem held shut while it sat in the Crown. With the Crown broken, the Accord is fraying: Fey mischief is turning up in parishes that have never had any, and the Verdant Court is restless.
- **The Host spares Millhaven.** The Heir has ordered his raiders to burn the western grain road and leave Millhaven untouched, because MAX is his uncle. Anyone mapping the burnings will see the hole in the pattern. See `world/factions/ashen-pretender.md`.
- **The lockbox.** At the Broken Antler, waiting for MAX's iron key, is the lockbox Marit left: her letters, the boy's hair, and the Last Sun's note to her father.
- **Hargrove's "grain."** The Old Houses' crates of crossbows and steel for the Host are marked as grain for Greenhollow. Some go through the western parishes on the way to the Frontier. A curious reeve could open one. See `world/factions/saltreach.md`.
- **The Long Barns are nearly empty.** The Granary books say Greenhollow's depot is half full and ready for winter. It's nearer a quarter. Factor Pell has been selling Granary grain quietly to the highest bidder and pocketing the difference, and he needs this harvest to cover the gap before Solace sends an auditor.

### What would change or destroy them

- **Change:** the Shire-moot actually agreeing. If Greenhollow spoke as one, it would be the most powerful voice at the Council of Banners that nobody has thought to invite.
- **Destroy:** a burned harvest, a broken Accord, or both in the same year.

## Hooks for one-shots

- **The Millhaven Harvest (Act I):** two armies want the harvest; someone is digging under the coliseum.
- **The Shire-moot (filler, any act):** the heroes are hired to keep the moot safe for three days while agents of every claimant work the delegates. Intrigue at farmhouse scale, and the vote decides who eats this winter.
- **The burning road (filler, Act I–II):** escort the last caravan up the pass road before the Host closes it. Or find out why the raiders keep skipping one village.
- **The Long Barns (filler, Act I–II):** a Solace auditor has gone missing at Wendle Cross. Factor Pell would like the heroes to find him, and would like them not to look in the barns.
- **Outside the hedge (filler, Act II–III):** the Fey come through somewhere other than Millhaven. A parish that never had an Accord has to make one by sundown.

## For the Ashen Pretender

**The weakness he'd exploit: they won't fight.** Greenhollow has no army and a habit of letting trouble pass its gate. He doesn't need to conquer the Shires: burning the grain road starves the Holds and doubles bread in Solace, and he can do it without fighting anyone. And he spares Millhaven, which is either mercy or sentiment, depending on who notices.

## Open questions for the DM

- Does the Shire-moot reach a decision on screen (a one-shot), or off screen between acts, logged in the ledger?
- Should Greenhollow get a seat at the Council of Banners? It would let the table answer "many free peoples or one empire?" on behalf of people who never got asked.
